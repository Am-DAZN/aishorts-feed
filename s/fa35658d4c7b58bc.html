New method lets CPUs reuse AI reasoning at a fraction of the cost

Large language models often solve the same kind of problem from scratch every time. Researchers say this is wasteful, because working out a solution is different from applying it. They introduce agentic reasoning caching, which turns an AI model's reasoning steps into a program called an ARC that runs on an ordinary computer chip (a CPU). Once a task has been reasoned through, new cases can be handled with little help from the language model. The team reports that ARCs perform on par with top language models on hard math and science tasks. They are also fully explainable, can keep being trained, and hold up when inputs change. Calling an ARC is 16 to 291 times cheaper than calling the source model.
