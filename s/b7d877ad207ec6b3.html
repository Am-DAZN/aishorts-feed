Old Image Generators Learn to Fill In Any Part of a Picture

Many AI image models build a picture one piece at a time, in a fixed order, like reading a page. That makes them poor at inpainting (filling a gap), outpainting (extending an image) or editing. Fixing this usually means training a new model from scratch, which costs about three times more computing power. This paper shows a cheaper way. The authors adapt existing models so they can generate in any order. They add special position information and a training plan that moves the model step by step to the new skill. Tested on four models, it beat MaskGIT on ImageNet inpainting (FID 6.21 versus 10.44, where lower is better). It adds 13 to 33 percent extra compute and keeps the standard inference tools working. Image quality on regular generation dips a little. Code is public.
