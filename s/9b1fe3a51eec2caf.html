Removing GPS from Car Data Doesn't Hide Where You Drove

Some insurance programs and open driving datasets say they protect privacy by leaving out GPS. A new paper shows this is not enough. Researchers released CANTraj, a dataset of 3,940 drives from 454 drivers that pairs car sensor signals with GPS paths, plus a benchmark to test attacks. They tested six methods that rebuild routes from speed and steering data matched to road maps. Adding steering to speed raised five-minute route recovery from 56% to 87% when the start and heading were known. An AI language model that picks between candidate routes recovered 79% of routes on test windows. The data and code are public, so others can test and improve privacy protections.
