New Map of Linear Neural Network Training Shows a Path to the Best Solution

Researchers studied the "loss landscape" of linear neural networks. This is a way to picture how a model's error changes as its settings change. Past work focused on critical points, which are flat spots in that landscape. But training often steers around those spots. The new paper introduces the "critical thalweg," a structure that includes all the critical points and shows the landscape on a larger scale. It also gives a geometric proof of a classic 1980s result by Baldi and Hornik, using simpler assumptions. The landscape can be drawn on a two-dimensional plane. Tests suggest gradient descent, the standard training method, is pulled toward this path. Along it, the error keeps falling until it reaches the lowest possible value.
