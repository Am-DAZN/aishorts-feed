PIPETTE Trains AI Agents to Resist Hidden Prompt Attacks

AI agents often read outside content, like web pages or emails. Attackers can hide instructions in that content to hijack the agent. This is called indirect prompt injection. Teams test for this by training attack models, then use the attacks they find to train defenses. This paper shows that high attack success does not always make defenders stronger. Two leading attack methods, RL-Hammer and PISmith, keep finding the same kinds of attacks, so extra wins teach the defender little. The authors introduce PIPETTE, which rewards attacks that cover a wider range of strategies and trains the agent on fresh attacks as it learns. On the AgentDojo and ASB benchmarks, defended agents reached near-zero success for static attacks, held up against strong adaptive attacks, and kept task performance close to the undefended agent.
