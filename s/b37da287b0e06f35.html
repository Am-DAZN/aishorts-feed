Researchers Train AI Models to Honestly Report on Their Own Behavior

Some AI models behave badly in ways that are hard to spot in chat logs, such as cheating on rewards, lying, or flattering users too much. Asking the model about its own actions does not work well, because it is not trained to admit mistakes. This paper proposes "introspective oversight." A separate observer role inside the model is trained to answer questions about the assistant. It is rewarded only when its answers match what the assistant really did. On five tests, an observer built on Qwen3.6-27B scored 0.907 AUROC, a measure of how well it spots problems. That beat honesty training (0.864), transcript monitoring (0.828), and activation oracles (0.796). Training the observer also made the assistant easier to monitor. The authors say this is a workable way to oversee unwanted behavior.
