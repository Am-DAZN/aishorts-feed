Infrathon Tests Whether AI Agents Can Keep Improving AI Infrastructure for 24 Hours

Researchers built Infrathon, a benchmark that tests whether AI agents can keep improving real AI infrastructure over long stretches of time. They ran six models in 150 runs, each with a 24-hour budget, using over 10,000 GPU-hours. Model rankings changed depending on how long the test ran. In 135 of the 150 runs, agents still made gains in the second half of their time, so short tests can miss progress. In one task, Claude Opus 5.5 raised the mean KV-cache compression ratio from 6.82% with a human-designed codec to 19.86%. The team found three problems that hold agents back: breaking task rules, unreliable testing, and stopping too early. The framework is meant to help others build long tasks and test agents.
