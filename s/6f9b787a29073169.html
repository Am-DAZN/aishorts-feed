Choosing an AI Reviewer: What It Costs to Prove You Picked Well

A new paper studies how a team can pick an AI reviewer to check draft answers, and how much checking is needed to prove the pick is nearly as good as the best option. Checking means buying verified answer keys, and sometimes the reviews too. The authors give exact rules and an algorithm that estimates the cheapest proof cost. They also show that finding such a proof can be far harder than the proof itself. In one example, a single purchase would be enough in hindsight, but the expected search cost is (n+1)/2. Tests used 2,000 MMLU questions, 896 numerical reports and 48 electricity forecast reports. On MMLU, targeted audits used 6.9% fewer keys than a fixed order, and chosen reviewers beat picking by plain accuracy by 2.94 percentage points. On numerical reports, the gains were small or zero.
