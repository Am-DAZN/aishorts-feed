31-Year-Old KLS Conjecture in Geometry Solved With Human-Directed AI Help

Researchers, including Xinzhi Zhang of Microsoft Research, say they have proved the KLS conjecture, a math problem posed in 1995. It asks whether a simple flat cut is nearly as good as any curved cut at splitting a high-dimensional shape or probability distribution at its narrowest point. The proof shows the gap stays within a fixed constant, no matter how many dimensions there are. This matters because the cuts affect how fast random walks can explore a distribution, which underpins sampling methods used in statistics, optimization and computer science. The team worked with AI agents in a process directed by humans. The AI explored more than 100 possible approaches, while the researchers judged, trimmed and redirected the search. Other researchers have checked the work, and a machine-checked formal verification followed.
