New benchmark finds AI lags humans in picking research ideas

Researchers built IdeaTaste, a test of "research taste," meaning the judgment about which problems and methods are worth pursuing. Language models read a snapshot of papers frozen at 2025 and wrote research proposals. Those proposals were then compared with what human communities actually published in 2026. The team tested six models across four research areas and more than 90,000 proposals. The AI reached about 26% of the human level at matching cited papers and 18% at matching ideas. Models could pick up a field's general tasks and methods, but rarely found the concrete approaches humans later used. More background context or more proposals did not help much. Expert reviewers also rated the AI proposals lowest on expected success. The authors say the main weakness is turning directions into workable plans. They released an open, regularly updated leaderboard.
