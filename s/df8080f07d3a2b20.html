Online seller learns when to bid and discount using sampling-based AI agent

Researchers built an AI agent for an online store selling a perishable product, like fresh flowers. The store pays for search ads, offers a bundle discount with a staple item, and shows how much stock is left. It starts without knowing how its bids affect clicks or how strong its market is. The agent keeps a running belief about the market, draws one guess from it each period, and acts on that guess. In a simulated florist, it lost the least revenue on average across markets. In weak markets, it gave up 12% to 23% of one season's revenue over 500 seasons, four to seven times less than drawing once per season. A planner using simple average estimates can stop bidding and never learn that bidding pays. This agent escapes that trap in about three seasons, versus fifty for the slower method.
