Sleep2Word Estimates Brain Activity During Sleep From Heart and Breathing Signals

Researchers built Sleep2Word, a system that estimates a full night of EEG (brain wave) data from signals like breathing, heart rhythm (ECG), or pulse (PPG). This could help sleep analysis when no EEG was recorded. The system turns each signal into "words" of different lengths, which shortens the night's data by up to 69.7%. A translator model then predicts the matching brain-wave words. It was trained on over 20,000 sleep studies from 9 datasets. The team reports it beats existing methods at rebuilding EEG spectrograms, sleep staging, arousal detection, and survival prediction. The learned words also appear to link different body signals in ways that make physiological sense.
