LookAnything model learns to follow where people are looking

Researchers have built LookAnything, an AI model that works out where a person is looking and what that means. Older gaze tools need extra inputs, like cropped head images or depth maps, which can add errors and slow things down. LookAnything does not need them. It can answer with a point, a box, a heatmap, or a written description. The team also made HoliGaze, a dataset and test set with 311,000 question-answer pairs covering 9 tasks. The paper says LookAnything beat 33 leading vision-language models on these tasks. It also stayed competitive with specialist tools on 5 standard tests. It worked on data it had not seen and ran on a real robot. This matters for robots and for studying social cues.
