EVAThink teaches video AI when to answer fast and when to think

Video AI models often see only a small set of frames from a clip. Sometimes those frames show enough to answer a question right away. Other times the clues are thin, and the model does better if it reasons step by step. EVAThink trains a model to tell the difference. Researchers built pairs of video views with the same question and answer: one rich in evidence, one sparse. They kept pairs where reasoning helped the sparse view. A two-stage reinforcement learning process then rewards the right choice of answer mode and later improves efficiency. A extra step stops the model from always picking one mode. With only 6.7K training samples, it beat its base model on video benchmarks at 32 and 64 frames, and topped strong baselines on average. Code, model and data are public.
