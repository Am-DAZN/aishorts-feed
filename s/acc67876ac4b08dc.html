New Adam Tweak Trusts Steady Gradients to Train Better on Noisy Labels

Researchers propose MGO-SC, a small change to Adam-style optimizers, the tools that decide how a neural network updates itself during training. Noisy data, such as wrong labels, can make these updates unstable. MGO-SC tracks whether each parameter's gradient keeps pointing the same direction. Steady ones are trusted more, and jumpy ones count for less. The first version shrank step sizes and did worse, so the team built a version that moves trust around without changing the overall step size. On CIFAR-10 with ResNet-18, it beat AdamW across clean and noisy labels, including 1.4 points better at 40% symmetric noise. It needs no extra math or filtering of samples. The authors share their code and raw logs.
