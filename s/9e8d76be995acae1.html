Extra Trainable Tokens Help Text Embedding Models Retrieve Better

Embedding models turn text of any length into a fixed-size list of numbers, called a vector, which is used for search. This squeezing can mix real meaning with leftover noise from individual words, including quirks from a behavior called attention sink. Researchers propose a simple fix. They add special trainable [DUMP] tokens to the start of the input during training. The model design, pooling method, and training loss stay the same. The new tokens learn through the usual retrieval training goal. They give the model places to absorb uninformative signals, so those signals interfere less with the final vector. Tests on standard retrieval and long-context benchmarks showed steady gains over finetuned baselines, for both encoder and decoder models. Analysis also showed the tokens change how the model pays attention to low-information words. The authors say this is a lightweight way to improve search embeddings.
