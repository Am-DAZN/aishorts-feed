Study Separates Bad Privacy Tests From Failed Defences in Split AI Training

In split training, a language model is divided between a user's device and a server, and only mid-way data is shared. Researchers reviewed four earlier privacy test campaigns to see which claims hold up. They found that tests can fail for reasons unrelated to the defence, such as missed data leaks, misaligned labels, or uncalibrated probes. Of 24 required probes in one 19-setup study, only four were valid. Those four still showed real privacy breaches in 13 setups. In a later pilot of seven redesigned defences, all seven were breached on one measure, and none passed the full privacy check. The authors also show that a shared-key wrapper guards against network eavesdroppers but not a compromised host. The work gives scripts to rerun every check.
