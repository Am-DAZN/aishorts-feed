DexDuet Teaches Two Robot Hands to Copy How Humans Touch Objects

Researchers built DexDuet, a method that teaches two-handed robots from a single human demonstration. Older methods mostly make the robot move the object along the right path. They often ignore how the human hands touched it, so robot fingers may grab the wrong spot or slide, making the grip unstable. DexDuet adds rewards that push the robot to keep the human's hand positions and contact points. It splits control into two matching policies, one per hand, that each see the other's state. A training method called A-RSI helps with hard parts of a task. Across four benchmarks, three simulators and five robot types, it improved object tracking by 11 to 41 points. In 122 paired runs with similar tracking, it kept human contacts better in 117.
