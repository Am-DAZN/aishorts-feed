Multiple-Choice Format Switches Off AI Refusals, Study Finds

Many safety tests for AI chatbots use multiple-choice questions. Earlier work showed that models refuse harmful questions less often in this format. This paper explains why, using the WMDP dataset of hazardous-knowledge questions and two models, Llama-3.1-8B and Qwen3-8B. In multiple-choice, the models lock onto an answer letter very early. In open-ended questions, the refusal builds up step by step. The models' internal signals also sit about 3 times further from the direction linked to refusal. When the researchers switched off the parts of the model that pick the answer letter, refusals went up. The authors conclude that multiple-choice safety scores mostly measure what a model knows, not whether its safety features work.
