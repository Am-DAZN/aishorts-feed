Manufacturers urged to turn factory know-how into shared 'industrial intelligence'

MIT Technology Review looks at how manufacturers can get more value from what they already know. It calls this 'industrial intelligence': a firm's know-how, work practices, production data and understanding of its business. Much of it sits with experienced workers or in single plants. The piece says connecting it can help more teams use it. It also describes 'physical AI', meaning systems that sense conditions, reason about them and act in the real world. One example is a robot adjusting to varied parts within safe limits. Toyota Industries linked paint shop data and reports analysis time fell from five days to under four hours. Its pilot cut related paint defects by about 25%. The article says data shows what is happening, while industrial intelligence adds context for what should happen and when people must step in.
