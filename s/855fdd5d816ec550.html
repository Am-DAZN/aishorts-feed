Simple Text Beats Fancy Agent Setups at Flagging Violent Video

Researchers tested how to build AI systems that flag violent video for human reviewers. They used several small tools that describe video, spot objects, tag sounds and transcribe speech. A language model then made the call. The team compared three ways of passing that information along: messages between agents, a structured evidence graph, and plain text read in one go. They also tested a single model that watches the raw clip. On 799 clips, plain text scored about the same as the single model (F1 0.967 versus 0.968). The graph and message methods did worse. Adding sound information helped, matching human annotators, who often relied on sound or speech. The agent pipeline used about three times the GPU time of the single model. The authors also flag two flaws in video describers that can mislead such systems.
