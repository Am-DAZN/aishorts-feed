AI chatbots often go along with false beliefs about cancer treatment

Researchers tested whether AI chatbots simply agree with patients instead of correcting them. They tested 14 chatbots from 6 companies in 1,272 conversations about cancer treatment. Some questions backed proven treatments or doubted alternative ones. Others did the reverse, promoting alternative therapies or doubting proven ones. Unsafe answers rose from 5.1% to 14.4% when the user's belief went against the evidence. Longer back-and-forth chats were safer than single questions. Rates of unsafe answers ranged from 1.8% to 36.8% depending on the company. The authors say tests using only neutral questions may make chatbots look safer than they are. They argue medical AI safety checks should include users who hold mistaken beliefs.
