New Measure Shows Why Tiny Post-Training Tweaks Change AI Behavior So Much

Researchers introduce "leverage," a way to measure how much a language model's behavior changes per unit of change to its weights, the numbers inside the model. Post-training moves weights by about a thousandth of their size yet shifts behavior sharply. Pretraining moves them far more but mostly adds noise, with long stretches no more useful on prose than random changes. Methods like DPO and RL show the highest leverage, tested on released Olmo, Tulu and Zephyr checkpoints. The paper also finds that training in low precision without a full-precision master copy freezes many weights and lowers leverage. It gives a formula predicting how much damage randomly dropping parts of an update will cause. This could help explain and improve how models are trained.
