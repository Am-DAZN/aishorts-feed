GlanceNav Lets Robots Follow Directions With Fixed-Cost Memory and Smarter Learning

Researchers built GlanceNav, an AI that follows spoken-style directions to move through a space using camera images. Many current models re-read past frames at every step, which wastes computation and limits how much history they can use. GlanceNav looks at each frame only once and keeps a fixed-size memory, so each step costs the same. It saves recent views and a few key frames that change its memory the most. The team also added a training method called Counterfactual Rewind. When the AI drifts off course, it is rewound to its closest point to the goal, an expert fixes a few actions, and the AI learns from the difference. A 4B-size model reached 71.9% and 73.6% success on two navigation tests, R2R-CE and RxR-CE. On R2R-CE it beat the strongest comparable method by 9.1 points, at 104 ms per step.
