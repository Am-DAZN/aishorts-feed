New Training Method Stops AI Agents From Answering Too Early

AI agents working on long tasks often give an answer before they have enough evidence. Researchers call this laziness. A reward given only for the final answer can't fix it, because a lucky guess scores the same as a well-supported answer. The new method, called RLVB, uses the training simulator to check at each step whether the evidence is already enough. It rewards the agent for gathering more clues when several answers are still possible, and for answering once the evidence is sufficient. It needs no judge model. Tested on three vision-language models and three benchmarks, it beat the base model in all nine settings, by 17.1% on average. It also beat a judge-based method by 9.3% while using 1.6 times fewer GPU-hours. The team also built Pro Worlds, a new set of visual puzzles.
