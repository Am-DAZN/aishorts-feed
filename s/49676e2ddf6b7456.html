OneSteer Lets Humanoid Robots Follow Motions While Adapting to New Obstacles

Researchers built OneSteer, a controller that makes a humanoid robot copy a reference motion while still adjusting to new goals. It uses a one-step diffusion model, a type of AI that generates actions from random noise. To change behavior, the system tweaks that noise instead of retraining the model. It can also relax tracking for chosen body parts. In simulation, the rate of clean passes under a 1.25 m bar rose from 2% to 90%. On a real Unitree G1 robot, it finished all 27 tracking trials without falling. It passed under a 1.1 m bar in four of five tries and stepped over a 12 cm hurdle in all three tries. It can also flag likely failures over 10 times faster than simulation.
