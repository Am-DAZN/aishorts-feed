Why AI Training Drifts Off Course, and a Fix for It

When teaching large language models with reinforcement learning, one program writes the answers and another program learns from them. The two often disagree slightly, which makes the training data less reliable. This paper finds two causes: small number differences in the answer-writing engine, and out-of-date model weights when sampling runs asynchronously, meaning at a different pace. The number differences stay steady, so they can be corrected. The out-of-date weights are worse, because each update feeds the next error in a loop. The authors propose a fix called DLC. It adjusts for the number differences, limits the lag error, and refreshes the sampler when sample quality drops too far. On Qwen3-0.6B and Qwen3-1.7B, DLC kept training close to standard on-policy training and prevented collapses that other fixes missed.
