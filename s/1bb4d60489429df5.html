LoRazor Speeds Up AI Serving by Dropping Custom Add-Ons Early

LoRA is a way to customize one big AI model for many users with small add-on pieces called adapters. Serving many users at once, each with a different adapter, is slow and uses a lot of memory. Researchers found that requests often do not need their adapter for the whole answer. Releasing it early barely hurts accuracy. Their system, LoRazor, decides when to drop a request's adapter and finish with the main model alone. It also reshapes GPU work so chips are not left idle. Across two models and four tasks, it handled up to 3.02 times more requests and 2.53 times more output tokens than vLLM multi-LoRA. The cost was a 2.89% average drop in task accuracy.
