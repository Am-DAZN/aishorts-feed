Study tests how trustworthy uncertainty is in brain activity mapping from M/EEG

M/EEG sensors record the brain's tiny magnetic and electric signals. Software then works out where in the brain they came from. Good tools should also say how sure they are, but those confidence ranges are rarely checked. This paper builds a simulation method to test them. It compares two approaches: Bayesian minimum norm and sparse Bayesian learning. Both were somewhat off in their confidence levels. A fix called isotonic recalibration, which adjusts the ranges using observed hit rates, improved both. The sparse method located sources more precisely and gave tighter uncertainty before the fix. On real auditory MEG data, it also gave sharper results for sources on both sides of the brain. How well the fix carries over to different conditions still varies.
