WorldDraft Tests Whether Video AI Can Simply Render Worlds Built by Code

Some AI coding agents now build a 3D world that moves over time, using code. A video model could then just draw it. A new paper asks if video models can do that job well. The team built WorldDraft, a test with 432 paired cases across 48 code-made worlds. It checks two things: does the video keep the planned motion and camera path, and does it show the text-described look? Tests on 12 models found a clash between the two goals. Richer drafts kept structure better but hurt the look. When the draft's look conflicted with the text, models copied the draft's look up to 64% of the time. The authors also propose WorldDraft-Primer, a visual prompting method that improved both goals on existing video models without retraining them.
