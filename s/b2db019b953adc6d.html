Audit finds over half of personal-data AI skills raise privacy concerns

Agent skills are add-ons that give AI assistants new abilities. They can reach a user's files, accounts and data. Researchers built SkillPrivus, a tool that checks a skill before installation without running it. It reads the code and instructions, finds how data is used, and judges whether that use is appropriate. They tested 726 real skills from public marketplaces. Of the 563 that handle personal data, 307 (54.5%) had at least one possible privacy violation. Problems included guessing sensitive facts about users, using information beyond the stated task, and sharing data with unexpected recipients. The tool's judgments matched three human reviewers 83.2% of the time on resolvable cases. The study suggests privacy harm can come from skills doing their normal jobs.
