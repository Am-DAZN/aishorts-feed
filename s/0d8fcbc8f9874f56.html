How People Really Spot AI Writing: Context Matters More Than Word Choice

Researchers studied how real people decide a text was written by AI. They looked at English Wikipedia editors and students, using hand-coded labels plus computer analysis of how language changes over time. They found people rely on many kinds of clues, from specific text features to guesses about an author's character. These clues often contradict each other and shift with context. Often, the trigger is not one word or phrase but a sense that something does not fit the setting. The authors say calling writing "AI-like" can also set rules about what is proper and even what counts as human. They warn that AI detection tools treating AI-likeness as the same everywhere may copy the norms of their training data and enforce them at scale.
