Best Way to Train Small Medical AI Depends on the Question Type

Small language models, with 9 billion parameters or fewer, can answer medical questions on a local device. That helps with privacy and speed. But training them well is hard. This paper tests two ways of teaching models to show their step-by-step reasoning before answering. One weights the whole reasoning-and-answer text equally. The other weights reasoning and answer separately. Neither wins everywhere. On multiple-choice tests, the separate weighting usually did better, and Qwen3.5-4B improved by up to 44.8% on MedQA over its untrained baseline. On free-text questions, equal weighting did best for nearly all models. A 3B Llama model came close to larger 7B medical models. Very long reasoning also seemed to hurt some models. The authors conclude that training should fit the task format and what the base model already knows.
