Small AI Models Learn Agent Skills Without Seeing Teacher's Reasoning

Researchers found a way to train small AI models to act like stronger ones, even when the strong model hides its step-by-step thinking. Many top models are closed, so their thinking is not available. The team trained students only on the teacher's actions, leaving the thinking block empty. Oddly, the skill still worked when the student later used its own reasoning. They call this cross-format transfer. On the AppWorld task test, Qwen3-4B rose from 7% to 18%. Adding short summaries of the teacher's reasoning lifted it to 35%. Using GPT-5.6 Luna as teacher, Qwen3-4B went from 7% to 48% on AppWorld and from 3% to 28% on SWE-bench Verified. This could make it cheaper to build capable small agents from closed models.
