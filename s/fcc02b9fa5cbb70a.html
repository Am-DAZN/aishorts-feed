Reframe Teaches Visual AI Agents to Actually Use Their Tools' Results

Some AI models look at images and videos and can call tools, like zooming or cropping, to help them answer questions. But they often ignore what the tools return. Bad tool actions also give weak material for training. Researchers built Reframe to fix this. First, specialist models learn to gather solid evidence, and sometimes the original image is removed so they must rely on tool results. Second, during training, poor tool actions are swapped for better standard ones mid-task, and learning continues from the fixed result. Neither step needs a separate early round of supervised training. Across 14 image and video tests, Reframe beat two other training methods. The team says the first step made models lean more on tool results.
