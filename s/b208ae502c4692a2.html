Study Shows What Really Drives AI Accuracy on Chip Wafer Defect Data

WM-811K is a well-known dataset of wafer maps, which are images showing which tiny chip parts (dies) failed on a silicon wafer. AI models called CNNs score high on it, but it was unclear why. Researchers cleaned the data, removing duplicates and conflicting labels, and kept 25,513 maps across eight defect types. They then made four versions of each map: the original, layout only, failed dies shuffled anywhere, and failed dies shuffled within distance rings from the center. Scores rose step by step: 0.3829, 0.5803, 0.7439, and 0.8563 for the original. So the layout, the number of failures, the distance from the center, and exact positions all carry useful signals. High scores do not come only from learning the exact defect pattern.
