New training reward helps AI mimic how different groups answer surveys

Researchers are using large language models to act as survey respondents in social-science studies. A good simulator should get individual answers right, match the spread of answers across a population, and reflect differences between demographic groups. Current training methods struggle to do all three. Rewarding exact matches pushes the model to pick the most common answer, which flattens the spread. Matching overall totals can hide the fact that the model ignores demographics. The team proposes Distributionally-Verifiable Rewards (DVR), a reinforcement-learning reward that uses gaps between demographic group distributions to guide each answer. On the OpinionQA dataset, DVR kept accuracy while cutting a distribution-gap measure (JSD) by 34.5% on familiar questions and 14.6% on unseen ones. The authors also show that a low JSD alone does not prove a model captures group differences, so accuracy, distribution fit, and demographic structure should be checked together.
