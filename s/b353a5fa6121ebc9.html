New decoding method cuts hallucinations in multimodal diffusion language models

Researchers studied multimodal diffusion language models, which build answers by repeatedly refining masked, or hidden, words. They found that the model's uncertainty usually falls as it works, but a few steps show sudden spikes. These spikes are closely linked to hallucinations, where the model states things that are not true. The team says it can spot such steps by comparing each one to its neighbors. Their fix, called StepRefine, needs no retraining. At spike steps, it keeps a weighted mix of possible words instead of locking in one choice, and refines this several times. A controller then switches back to normal decoding once things settle. Tests on multimodal benchmarks showed clear drops in hallucinations across different models.
