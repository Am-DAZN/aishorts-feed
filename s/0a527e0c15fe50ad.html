AI Learns to Describe Heart Murmurs, Not Just Detect Them

Doctors listening to a heart do more than spot a murmur, an extra whooshing sound. They also note its timing, shape, pitch, quality and loudness. Most computer tools only say whether a murmur is present. This study tests ways to describe it in more detail. Researchers used heart recordings from four chest spots for 735 patients. The best method trained a model at each spot and combined the results, picking the most severe finding. It scored 85.56% to 93.81% average sensitivity across five tasks. Sensitivity means how often real cases are caught. Two neural networks that learn from all four spots together did worse. One trailed by 7 to 16 points. The other, which predicts all five traits at once, scored 72.59% to 79.40%. Adding all extra examples of rare cases to balance the data hurt results, since it brought in noisy sound segments.
