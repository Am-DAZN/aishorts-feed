New ECG Privacy Method Hides Age and Sex While Keeping Diagnosis Useful

An ECG heart recording can reveal a person's age and sex, not just heart problems. Researchers tested TIBECG, a method that squeezes the signal through a narrow bottleneck so less personal information gets through. They tested it on 6,386 hospital recordings, looking at each of the 12 ECG leads separately. The method kept at least 95% of diagnostic accuracy on every lead. It also hid age and sex better than the best standard processing method, by a median of 22.08 percentage points. Simply lowering the sampling rate often hid more personal data, but it hurt diagnosis more. Results varied by lead. The authors say this is not ready for consumer use, and it does not produce signals a doctor can read. It matters when ECG data must be sent to a cloud service for analysis.
