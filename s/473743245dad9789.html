Zugwise Tackles Silent Errors When Digitizing Handwritten Chess Scoresheets

Turning handwritten chess scoresheets into digital games is usually treated as a handwriting-reading task. Researchers say that misses the worst mistakes. A misread move that is illegal is easy to spot. A misread that is legal but wrong goes unnoticed and throws off every later move. Zugwise treats the job as rebuilding the whole game. It borrows chess-engine tools to ask whether a human could plausibly have written a move, and it traces late problems back to their cause. It also merges the second player's sheet and sends disagreements to a person. In tests on two tournament sections, silent errors fell from 16 to 1 and from 50 to 13. The tool runs free in a web browser, even offline. The authors do not claim it is faster or more accurate than other workflows.
