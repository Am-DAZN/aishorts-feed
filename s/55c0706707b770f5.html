Language Models Write Brain and Heart Signal Networks Without Training

Researchers tested "weight-space programming," where a language model reads a neural network's weights and edits them, instead of the usual step-by-step training called gradient descent. They applied it to networks that read EEG (brain) and ECG (heart) signals. Top models could find hidden faults without being told about them. They could also write all of a network's weights from scratch, given only its design, the task, and a few training subjects. In one test, a model matched normal training using 6 times fewer subjects. Networks written by Claude Opus 5.5 beat classic hand-built EEG methods when both used the same 5 subjects. There are limits. The models could not improve networks that were already well trained, and could not reliably tell what task a network was trained for. The authors say this may suit small networks on wearables, but needs more study.
