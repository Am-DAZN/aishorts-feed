Hospitals train shared AI to grade lung cancer without sharing patient data

Doctors often disagree when grading lung adenocarcinoma, a common lung cancer, which makes consistent diagnosis hard. Sharing patient data between hospitals is also limited by privacy rules. This study built a web-based system using federated learning, where each hospital trains a model locally and shares only the model's learned settings, not patient records. It used a pathology foundation model called UNI2 to read tissue slide images. The team compared four federated methods. FedNova gave the most balanced results between the two cancer grades. The team also trimmed the models, a step called pruning, to shrink what gets sent between sites. With FedNova, pruning raised accuracy scores. The authors say FedNova with 40% pruning is a practical choice that cuts communication costs while protecting privacy.
