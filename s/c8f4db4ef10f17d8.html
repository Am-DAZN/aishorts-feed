Researchers Prove Platonic Representation Idea, Show Text JEPA Beats Giant Language Models

A popular idea in AI says that models trained on different kinds of data, like text and images, end up seeing the world in the same way as they grow. Until now, nobody had proven it. This paper turns the idea into a theorem. It shows that models called JEPAs, which learn by matching two views of the same scene, arrive at the same representation up to one rotation. The authors say next-token prediction, the method behind most chatbots, uses weaker views, so it lines up with vision only at large scale. In tests, a 109M-parameter text JEPA matched image models better than language models up to 72B parameters. Good views needed 42 times fewer training samples. The authors conclude that good views, not scale alone, are the key.
