RoboTryOn Turns Human Videos Into Robot Training Demos

Teaching a robot a new task usually needs people to control it by remote, which is slow and costly. RoboTryOn offers another way. It takes a video of a person doing a task and turns it into a video of a chosen robot doing the same thing, with the same objects and background. The idea works like virtual clothes try-on: keep the motion, swap the human for the robot. The system uses the hand skeleton to guide the motion and a model that works out the robot's actions from the video. In tests on several humanoid robots, success on new tasks, including two-handed ones, rose from 2% to 60% with no real robot demonstrations of those tasks. It also matched remote-control training at about a third of the collection cost.
