Deleting 'stale' facts from AI memory can backfire, study finds

Many AI agents keep a memory and delete facts they judge to be out of date. Researchers tested what happens when that judgment is wrong. If the system deletes a fact the question needs, accuracy can fall sharply. In tests on three models, deleting helped when the judgments were perfect, but hurt when they were least reliable. Wrongly deleting a fact did more harm than wrongly keeping one. Even a method that keeps everything can fail if bad labels mislead the answer. The authors tested a simple gate that decides when to delete, and it scored 0.655 accuracy, against 0.641 for always deleting. They advise checking the risk of wrong deletions on matched data first. If that is not possible, keep the facts.
