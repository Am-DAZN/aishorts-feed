AI Radar Vehicle Recognition Often Relies on Background, Not the Vehicle

Deep networks score near 99% on MSTAR, a well-known test for spotting vehicles in radar images. A new study asks how much of that success comes from the vehicle itself. With the center of each image blanked out, hiding the vehicle, a modern network still got 73.3% right on MSTAR. On a newer, harder benchmark called ATRNet-STAR, it got 63.3% right with the vehicle removed. So the background is a shortcut. When the scene changes, that shortcut hurts instead, and accuracy falls to 22.8%. A radar foundation model, SARATR-X, leaned on the background even more. The study also found that accuracy depends mainly on how far the test viewing angle is from the nearest training angle. At a 15-degree gap, accuracy was 57%. At 45 degrees, it was 26%. The authors urge researchers to report background-only and vehicle-only tests, plus the angle gap, with any accuracy score. Code is public.
