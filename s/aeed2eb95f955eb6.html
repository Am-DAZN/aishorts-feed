SWAM Study Shows How to Keep Robot AI Grounded in Language

Some AI models, called world action models, predict video and robot actions from a text instruction. The authors say learning actions may weaken the link between language and video that the model learned earlier. To test this, they gathered about 47,000 hours of video, including general, first-person, UMI and robot demonstrations. They found that mixing first-person videos with UMI demonstrations helped robots handle new situations. On the RoboTwin benchmark, the model scored 88.10% on easy tests and 61.56% on hard ones. In three real-world tasks it succeeded 40.00% of the time, versus 23.33% for the best baseline. The team is releasing the data, training code and model weights.
