Hidden Residual Gates May Explain Faster Diffusion Model Training

Some methods, like REPA and dispersive regularization, help image-generating diffusion transformers train faster. The usual explanation is extra supervision or better feature structure. This paper points to something else. These methods reshape a small internal control called the residual gate, which decides how much of each update enters the model. Accelerated models react more strongly when this gate is disturbed. The authors also measure how many channels the gate uses, and find that wider use goes with better image quality. Based on this, they propose a simple training objective that needs no teacher model and encourages wider gate use. On ImageNet 256x256 with SiT-B/2, it beats dispersive loss on FID, a score where lower means better images. The work suggests gate organization is a measurable key to faster training.
