New test suite checks how well AI handles crisis counseling chats

Crisis helplines often lack staff and money. AI chatbots could help counselors, but current tests miss key skills like judging risk and calming a person down. Real call transcripts are private, which makes testing hard. Researchers built CrisisBench, a test suite that protects privacy. It uses 1,085 single-turn tasks from 200 de-identified conversations, plus 1,185 simulated patients built from hotline metadata. Five GPT models were tested. All did worse than human counselors on showing too much empathy and asking too many questions. In longer chats, a retrieval-based system scored highest at 0.961, and all systems were close. Safety and staying productive caused most of the differences. Counselors rated the simulated patients realistic (86%) and useful (92%). The team is releasing the rubrics, judge prompts and tasks.
