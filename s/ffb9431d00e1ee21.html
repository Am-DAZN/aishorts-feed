Paper argues AI models may not need long context windows

A new research paper questions whether language models really need to handle very long inputs. The authors note that later words in a long context cost more to generate than earlier ones. On leading open-weight models, that gap can reach 7 times. They tested a method called frequent compaction, which keeps the context short by regularly condensing it. Across many long tasks, top models lost little or no accuracy. The team also found that performance did not collapse on long software engineering tasks, even when the model took at most one action per context. Based on this, they propose semantic state space models, a new idea for how to build such systems. The results challenge the need for long-context models.
