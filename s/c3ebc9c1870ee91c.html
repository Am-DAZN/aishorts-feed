Stress Test Shows AI Models Often See Cause and Effect That Isn't There

Researchers built a stress-test framework to check how well large language models (LLMs), the AI behind chatbots, can work out what causes what. They tested six areas: model size, how complex the cause-and-effect map is, prompting style, quality of descriptive labels, data quality, and handling of uncertainty. The models showed an "optimism bias": they kept finding causal links that were not real, so their precision was low. Common scoring methods also looked too kind, because top scores came when uncertainty was ignored. Once uncertainty was counted, scores fell. Bigger models did better, but the gains were fragile and dropped sharply when labels and descriptions were poor. The paper offers a practical guide to when LLMs can help scientists find causes and when they may mislead them.
