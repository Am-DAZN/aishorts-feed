Study finds AI image tools show human values in narrow, repetitive ways

Researchers studied how text-to-image AI models turn ideas like success, care or freedom into pictures. They used 70 value-based prompts, based on Schwartz's theory of basic human values, and made 28,000 images with four leading models. They compared the images with how people describe the same values. The models gave far narrower results than people did. They leaned toward a very individual, adult-focused view of the world. Achievement often appeared in formal institutional settings, and hedonism (pleasure-seeking) often looked like wealthy shopping. Everyday work, caring relationships and children's views were mostly left out. The team also showed a proof of concept: using these gaps to steer the models toward more varied images without changing the user's prompt. The work matters because these tools shape everyday visual culture.
