Different Goals Help AI Agents Learn Together Without Extra Exploration

Federated reinforcement learning lets many AI agents learn together while sharing what they find. It usually works best when all agents want the same thing. This paper argues that agents with different goals can actually help each other. Because their goals differ, they visit a wider range of situations. The authors first present an algorithm called PF-UCBVI, which speeds up learning in line with the number of agents. They then present PF-EFVI, a purely greedy method. Greedy means it always picks the best-looking action and never adds bonuses to push itself to try new things. The authors prove it keeps its mistakes low, at a logarithmic rate, as long as the agents' goals are different enough. The takeaway is that disagreement between agents can replace deliberate exploration.
