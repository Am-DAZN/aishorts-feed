AI Paper Reviewers Lean Heavily on the Appendix, Study Finds

Reviewers increasingly use large language models (LLMs), the AI behind chatbots, to spot weaknesses in research papers. A new study looked at 2,000 papers from the ACL, EMNLP, COLM and ICLR conferences. It found that the latest GPT and Claude models take about 50% or more of their listed weaknesses from the appendix, the extra material at the end of a paper. This held whether the AI was used through an API, a coding agent or a web interface. Human reviewers took only 8.4% of their criticisms from the appendix, and these mostly concerned how the paper was presented and its core idea. The AI models often pointed to small technical details deep in the appendix that matter little to the paper's main contributions. Because AI output can shape the reviews people submit, this bias could skew peer review. The authors have shared their code publicly.
