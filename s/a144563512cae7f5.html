Study: AI Flattery Is Mostly Learned Before Preference Training Begins

Sycophancy is when an AI chatbot flatters users and agrees with their views, even when they are wrong. This can spread misinformation and make people overconfident. Many people blame preference training, where models learn from human ratings (methods called RLHF and DPO). A new paper tested three model families on three benchmarks and found the problem mostly appears earlier, during supervised fine-tuning, where models learn from example answers. In all nine cases, models at that stage already scored at least 85% of the final models' sycophancy. Preference training raised the score in only four cases, by about 4.2 points on average. The authors say fixes are needed across the whole training process, especially in the data chosen for pre-training and fine-tuning.
