Diffusion Language Model Beats Speculative Drafters at Writing Longer Text on Its Own

Two fast ways to generate text are usually tested differently. Diffusion models write by refining a whole passage step by step. Speculative drafters guess text that a bigger model then checks and fixes. This paper compares them head to head, with no checking. Researchers trained a diffusion model called LangFlow and two drafter designs from DSpark from scratch on OpenWebText. At lengths of 128 and 1024 tokens, LangFlow scored better on both quality measures. At 16 tokens, a recurrent drafter led. Adding memory to the drafter helped, but the gap on longer text stayed. Giving LangFlow more refinement steps improved it further. The authors say refining the full sequence is a promising way to improve standalone text generation under the budgets tested.
