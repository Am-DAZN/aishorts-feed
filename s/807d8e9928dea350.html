DreamX turns a video AI model into a universal action learner for robots

Researchers introduced DreamX, a method that reuses a pretrained video diffusion model, an AI that generates video, to learn "latent actions." These are compact codes that describe what is happening in a video, like a reach or a grasp. Older methods often mix the action with how things look, so they transfer poorly between humans and robots. DreamX squeezes each video frame from 1,200 tokens down to 4, about 300 times smaller, and keeps the action while dropping appearance. The authors report top results among latent action models on LARY-Bench. The same codes also work across different bodies and settings, including moving actions between videos and zero-shot transfer. They can also make robot policies cheaper to run.
