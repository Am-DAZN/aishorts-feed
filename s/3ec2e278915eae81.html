Data center backlash may be more about electricity than culture war

MIT Technology Review's newsletter visited Ashburn, Virginia, the world's largest data center hub. OpenAI's Chris Lehane says data centers are a stand-in for public worries about costs and corporate power. The writer disagrees and says the anger is mostly about electricity. Most Virginia sites use the local grid, which relies heavily on natural gas. The average one draws 54 megawatts, more than Denver's airport. Activist Elena Schlossberg says it was always about power. Protests led to a state rule that data centers pay the full cost of new power lines. Elsewhere, huge AI sites in rural areas build their own gas turbines, and Anthropic has reportedly agreed to pay $3.4 billion for some. The industry can improve its image, but not escape its power demands.
