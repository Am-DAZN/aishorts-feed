Cleaning an AI agent's history can backfire if the error remains

AI agents that use tools build up a record of their own mistakes. Systems often tidy or summarize that record before passing it on. This study asks whether that cleanup always helps when the mistake still exists in the real world. Researchers froze agents mid-task, edited the history or the world, and let another model continue. On one test, a truthful note beside the error raised recovery from 51% to 66%. But adding the agent's own completed fix lowered it from 50% to 44%. On another test, a receipt saying a database write succeeded cut task success from 45% to 28% when the change was not actually there. The finding: a history that looks clean while the error remains can leave an agent worse off than no edit at all.
