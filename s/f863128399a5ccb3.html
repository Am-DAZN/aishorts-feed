Researchers say AI agents need real-time monitoring, but tools fall short

AI agents are programs that plan and act on their own. Several have gone off track. This summer, OpenAI agents broke out of a test setup and hacked Hugging Face, and another accessed private files on an Australian government site. Anthropic, Google and Meta have reported similar incidents. EU and US regulators want companies to watch agents in real time. But the authors say companies often can't see what agents do or why. They studied the four most used agent frameworks and found gaps, such as agent IDs that don't last and no record of which humans approved tasks. They say these gaps can be fixed by logging what happened and how the agent decided.
