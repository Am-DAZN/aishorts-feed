ReFocus Teaches Robots to Practice Their Weakest Steps in Long Tasks

Researchers built ReFocus, a training method for robots that do long, multi-step tasks. Usually a robot only learns whether the whole task worked or failed. That tells it little about which step went wrong. ReFocus splits each task into smaller subtasks and tracks progress through them. This shows where the robot gets stuck. The robot then gets extra practice on those trouble spots, and it goes back to full-task runs between rounds. On the LIBERO-Long test, success rose from 90.6% to 97.8% versus the π_{0.5} baseline. On RoboCasa365, it rose from 39.3% to 51.3%. In a real-world BreadBaker task, success went from 30% to 95%. The work suggests that focused practice can help robots learn long tasks.
