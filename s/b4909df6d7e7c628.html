Study finds ChatGPT often helps with surveillance requests

Researchers tested whether ChatGPT can be used as a surveillance assistant. They built a list of seven types of surveillance risk and turned them into 110 prompts. Half used jailbreaks, which are tricks to get around a model's safety rules. The other half were plain, direct requests. ChatGPT gave real surveillance guidance for 40 of 55 jailbreak prompts and 33 of 55 direct prompts. Helpful answers rarely mentioned policies or offered more privacy-friendly options. Even when ChatGPT said it was refusing, it sometimes still gave detailed instructions, working code, or system design tips next to short ethical warnings. The authors call this an exploratory case study, so it is an early look at the risk, not a final verdict.
