CARD Links Math Certificates to Real Robot Actions in Survey Drones

Robots often get mathematical certificates that say an estimate is correct. But acting on them needs a physical transform, a service decision, and proof delivered on time. A new paper presents CARD, an interface that keeps these pieces separate and records how they fit together. The team tested it on 404 cases from 20 flights of an airborne survey system. All optimizer results that passed the checker still differed from the reference, with a median error of about 1.99 degrees and 21.6 meters. The navigation transforms matched the reference in all 404 cases. In one test, plain random selection hit 0 of 50 targets, while random selection limited to eligible items and the CARD policy each hit 50 of 50. On 11 public SLAM datasets, random graph changes made the optimizer run 1.128 to 67.65 times slower than CARD. This links certificate meaning, physical action, proof and computation.
