New Method Helps AI Read Long Videos and Books Faster

Large AI models struggle with very long inputs like novels or hour-long videos. They slow down, make more mistakes, and hit a hard limit on how much they can read. A recent trick called grid probing helps by asking the model the same question about small pieces of the input. But it repeats the full work for every piece, which is costly. Researchers now present Factorized Context Attention, or FCA. It needs no extra training. It reads the input once, saves it in memory, and checks all the pieces together in one go. It works with existing frozen models. The team reports about 10 points more accuracy on long video and 9 to 14 points on long novels. It also finds hidden details in videos over a thousand frames long. Later questions on the same input can run in a tenth of the time.
