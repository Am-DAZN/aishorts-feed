Training-free method reads heart rate from face video more accurately than a common baseline

Researchers introduced SAMA, a method that estimates heart rate from ordinary face video. It needs no training and no tuning for each dataset. It combines two standard signal methods, filters out frequencies that come from motion or lighting changes, and tracks the pulse over time. It also gives a confidence score for each short window. Tested on 218 recordings from five public datasets, it had lower average error than the common POS method: 5.05 against 6.59 beats per minute on 10-second windows. The gain was statistically clear on short windows but not per video, and it did not win on every dataset. Dropping the 20% least confident windows cut error from 4.06 to 2.33. The authors say it does not beat the best neural networks on one dataset, UBFC-rPPG. They also argue for a skin-tone-balanced data collection in India.
