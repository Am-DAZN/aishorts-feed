FastFrames Makes AI Video Generation Fast Enough for Real-Time Interaction

Some AI video models create each new frame based on what a user just did. This makes them useful for interactive experiences, but today's models are far too slow. A new paper introduces FastFrames, a family of faster models of this kind. The authors measured where time was being spent and found that two steps, turning compressed data back into images (VAE decoding) and updating the model's memory of past frames (KV cache updates), took over half of the total time. They replaced both with lighter alternatives. The 1.3B-size model makes a frame in 36 milliseconds on one B200 GPU, and matches the quality of a leading model that is nearly 8 times slower. The 14B model takes 45 milliseconds per frame on eight B200 GPUs and looks better and more realistic. The team shows it working in camera-controlled and physics-controlled video.
