New robot test shows thinking and acting skills don't combine well

Researchers built Mind2Act World, a simulated test for robots that handle objects. It has 30 tasks from five real-world areas, in 180 setups. The tasks are split into three views: Mind tests decisions based on past events, Act tests precise and timely movement, and Mind2Act needs both, with results guiding later choices. This shows whether a robot lacks a skill or just can't combine skills it has. The best AI agent succeeded in 52.7% of Mind and 39.0% of Act episodes, but only 12.0% of Mind2Act ones. Agents remember the task but struggle to keep steady contact with objects. End-to-end models repeat practiced motions but forget task progress. The authors say combining reasoning and action through feedback is the main open problem.
