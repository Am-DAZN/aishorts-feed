Paper asks why humans should do science if AI does it better

A new paper asks why people should keep doing science as AI matches or beats human researchers. It argues that a key goal of science is lasting, shared human understanding of the world, serving human values. From this, it builds a two-part test for handing a task to AI. First, the science community must still be able to explain, defend and challenge the results, and keep the skills to do so. Second, accountable humans must decide which questions matter and what findings mean. The authors apply the test to each stage of research, using a standard list of research roles. They say it still works even if AI becomes better than humans, and it judges each use of AI by its effect on human understanding. It also covers peer review and training new researchers, and ends with reforms to keep science human-centered.
