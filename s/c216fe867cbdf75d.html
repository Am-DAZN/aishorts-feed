Language model writes code that edits a neural network's weights to add new skills

Researchers introduced WeightCode, a way to give a trained neural network new abilities without retraining it. A frozen language model writes small programs that directly change the network's weights, the numbers that hold what it has learned. Once installed, the new skill runs on its own, with no language model needed. In tests, a network went from 0% to 99.8% exact match on new command combinations, and from 0% to 98.4% success on a new pickup action, while keeping its old skills. On ImageNet, a standard image test, the programs beat eight fine-tuning methods under matched conditions. The programs also worked on five other separately trained Vision Transformers, while simply copying the weight edits did not help. This suggests skills could be added to models step by step through reusable programs.
