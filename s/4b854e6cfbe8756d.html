Letting Each Graph Node Pick Its Own Mix of Layers Improves Long-Range Reasoning

Graph Transformers are AI models that learn from networks of connected points, called nodes. They are good at passing information between far-apart nodes. But when they combine the results from different layers, they add them up the same way for every node. Researchers say this fixed recipe can be a poor fit, since useful layers may differ from node to node. They propose Attention Residuals, which let each node weight the layers' outputs on its own. A block version cuts the cost for deep models. They added it to two models, GPS and GRIT, and tested on five ECHO tasks. Both versions had lower error than their original models on every task. At least one scored best overall on each task. Extra tests back the idea that the fixed mix was a problem.
