Math Rule Shows When Tiered AI Serving Actually Saves Money

Some systems split AI work across levels. A small model on a phone or nearby device answers easy questions. Hard ones go up to a big model in a datacenter. The choice depends on how confident the small model is. This paper gives a formula for when no confidence cutoff can make this setup worth it compared with sending everything to the big model. It uses a cost ratio between local and escalation costs. If local computing is billed, cost is the main limit. If local hardware is already paid for, as on decentralized hardware, the setup can always work, and the quality of the confidence signal decides how well it does. The authors list three design requirements and suggest a new architecture and test protocol as future work. The formula is the main contribution.
