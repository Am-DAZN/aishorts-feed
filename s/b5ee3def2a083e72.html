New AI method sharpens mmWave wireless channel estimates with fewer test signals

Researchers propose FACE, a way to estimate the state of wireless channels in millimeter-wave MIMO systems, which use many antennas and very high radio frequencies. This is hard because the channels are large, the test signals (pilots) are limited, and measurements are noisy. FACE uses flow matching, a type of generative AI that learns how to move from simple random data to realistic data. It also uses the fact that these channels are sparse when viewed by angle. In tests, FACE beat standard methods and a diffusion-based AI method on accuracy. It worked well with fewer pilots and reached data rates close to the ideal case of perfect channel knowledge. It also needed far fewer sampling steps than diffusion, which cuts computing time and cost.
