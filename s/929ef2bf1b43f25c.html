HairDeck Builds Game-Ready Stylized Hair From a Single Image

Characters in games and avatars often use hair cards. These are flat ribbons placed by artists, and they are moved by simple spring-like bones. HairDeck is a new method that makes them from one picture. Older tools made realistic hair or one solid mesh. The only earlier card generator, CHARM, built hair one point after another, which is slow. HairDeck treats a hairstyle as an unordered set of cards, with each card as a single token. A diffusion model, a type of AI that cleans up noise step by step, shapes all the cards together. It also makes the spring-bone chains that move them, so the result can be animated and exported to common formats. On CHARM's test set, it cut Chamfer distance, a measure of shape error, by 44%. It more than halved card-orientation error and ran 33 times faster.
