Robots Learn Faster by Choosing How Long to Run Each Plan

Many modern robot policies plan long chunks of actions, sometimes dozens of steps at once. Improving them with reinforcement learning, which is trial-and-error training, usually needs many real-world attempts. That is costly for robots. This paper introduces ExRL, short for Execution at the Right Length. Instead of tuning the actions themselves, it adds a small adapter that decides how long to carry out each planned chunk before planning again. This keeps the problem small, while the options grow with chunk size. The adapter is trained with off-policy RL, a method that can reuse past data. The authors report that ExRL adapted a range of long-chunk policies with few samples and little tuning, in both simulation and real robot tasks.
