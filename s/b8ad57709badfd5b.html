Study Tests Whether Vision AI Models Keep Track of Unseen Space

Researchers asked whether visual foundation models, large AI systems trained to understand images, can hold a lasting sense of space. People do this when they remember what lies outside their view. The team tested ten models on video from a moving viewpoint in static indoor rooms. They checked four skills: spatial structure, object persistence, predicting what comes next, and using space to guide action. Results were uneven. A model that read the visible scene well often failed to remember objects or predict future views. Predicted features also kept spatial information, but a reader trained on real features did not work well on them. Choosing actions for a goal was not reliable either. The authors say spatial AI should be judged not just by what it can decode, but by whether it remembers, predicts and supports action.
