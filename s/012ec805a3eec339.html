New Method Helps Language Models Give More Varied Answers

Language models often write good single answers, but when asked for several, the answers tend to repeat the same idea. This paper offers a way to pick a set of answers that are both good and different from each other. The method, called PoolDPP, first generates a pool of candidate answers. It then favors higher-quality ones and uses a statistical tool called a determinantal point process to choose a set that avoids near-duplicates. The authors prove that the chosen set gets closer to the ideal one as the pool grows. In tests, it improved the balance of quality and variety in open-ended writing and in ambiguous text-to-SQL tasks. On math problems from GSM8K, it gave more varied answers while solving about as many problems.
