FLAMIA speeds up AI attention training on NVIDIA Blackwell chips

Researchers built FLAMIA, a set of attention kernels (small programs that run on the GPU) for NVIDIA Blackwell chips. Attention is a costly step in training Transformer models, especially with long text. FLAMIA uses low-precision numbers to do the math faster. It also guards against a problem where shared scaling values can leak information about future words. It keeps the forward and backward passes consistent, and handles packed documents of different lengths without repacking. On causal packed-document tests, the MXFP8 version ran the combined forward and backward attention step 1.10 to 1.40 times faster on B200 and 1.06 to 1.42 times faster on B300 than the fastest baseline tested. The team also checked that training stayed stable and quality held up when pretraining Llama-style models.
