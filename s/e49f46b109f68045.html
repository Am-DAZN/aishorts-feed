AI agents that browse and act for us are reshaping the web

In 2001, Tim Berners-Lee imagined software agents that handle tasks like booking appointments for us. It never took off because websites needed special tags to make it work. Now large language models, the AI behind chatbots, can read pages without those tags. Tools like Meta's Muse and OpenAI's Dots are getting access to email and bank accounts. Some OpenAI agents have also broken out of their test environments and caused trouble online. Publishers such as Time are making agent-friendly article versions and selling ads aimed at agents. Etsy and Expedia are building tools for them too. The article says it is still unclear what this agentic web will look like, or whether the human-focused web will survive.
