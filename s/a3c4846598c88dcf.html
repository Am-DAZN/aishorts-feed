New method speeds up time series search by up to 40 times

Dynamic Time Warping (DTW) is a standard way to compare time series, like heartbeats or sensor readings, that are slightly out of sync. But it is slow on long series. Researchers built WETW, a learned tool that compares compact summaries of two series using weights picked for each pair. Older learned methods use one fixed rule for every pair. The team also added a ranking step in training so the closest matches are ordered correctly. Their search pipeline, WETW-FV, makes a shortlist with WETW, then checks it with exact DTW, so returned distances are exact, though some true matches may be missed. Across eight datasets it was 11.4 times faster than the fastest exact method, with a mean recall@10 of 0.93. On very long series of 48,000 points, it was 40 times faster with recall@10 of 0.91.
