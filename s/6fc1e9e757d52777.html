LipsFlower Makes AI Image Restoration More Stable Against Small Input Changes

Flow-matching models can repair damaged images, such as blurry or noisy faces, with high quality. But they have a weakness. Tiny changes to the input can get magnified and make the repaired image unstable. Researchers introduce LipsFlower to reduce this problem. They worked out math bounds showing how stable two common approaches are. They then built LipHDiT, a transformer network designed to limit how much it magnifies small changes. They also added a training penalty that discourages the model from being too sensitive to its inputs. The team tested the method on blind face restoration, where the type of damage is unknown. LipsFlower cut the amplification of small perturbations while keeping image quality competitive with the baselines they compared against. The results suggest that building stability controls into these models can make AI image restoration more reliable.
