Auditing a CV sorter shows where its early success was misleading

Academic job openings can draw hundreds of applications, and screening them eats up committee time. Researchers built a CV Priority Sorter for a national academic jobs platform. It suggests a reading order, but the committee makes every decision. On 300 synthetic CVs, reading in the tool's order found 95% of relevant candidates after half the pile. The team then audited its own claim and found problems. The test pool could be sorted by document length alone. The agreement score followed the model's confidence, not its ranking. Asking the committee about uncertain CVs raised total reading work. The authors report the fixes each problem forced. They also found that a local model can match a hosted one at the start, without sending data out.
