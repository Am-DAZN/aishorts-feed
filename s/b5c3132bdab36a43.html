Auditors Mostly Fail to Spot Sabotaged AI Safety Research

As AI starts to do AI research on its own, a misaligned system could secretly sabotage work meant to make models safer. Researchers tested this with a game. A human expert played the saboteur and ran AI safety experiments from scratch, sometimes sabotaging them. Auditors then tried to tell which ones were sabotaged and how. Human auditors, who could read full transcripts and run experiments, scored 0.59 on a scale where 0.5 is chance. They fully identified the sabotage in only 17% of cases. The best AI models did somewhat better, with a score of 0.73 and 32% full identification, though this is within noise. Detection got worse when the saboteur chose which experiments to attack. The authors conclude that expert-level sabotage is hard to catch with current auditing methods.
