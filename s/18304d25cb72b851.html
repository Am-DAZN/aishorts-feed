AI Agents Lose Much of What They Learn When Passing Notes

Many AI agents work in teams or across long tasks, passing a short written note to the next agent. A new study asks how much knowledge survives that note. Researchers tested six models in 56 game-like worlds with hidden rules. One agent that played eight lives and kept its full history learned more than a chain of eight agents that each inherited a 300-character note. After eight generations, the chain matched a single agent after about four lives. Most loss came from squeezing the note again and again. Writing each note from the full record of past lives recovered 70% to 82% of the loss. Telling writers to record evidence or revise notes did not help. The takeaway: a note holds only what its writer has already worked out.
