New AI Model Learns Stock Behavior by Factoring in Market-Wide Shocks

Much of what moves a stock comes from sudden market-wide shocks that its own price history cannot predict. Most AI methods treat this as random noise. Researchers propose Market-Conditioned JEPA, a model that uses the movement of other stocks during training to separate market effects from a stock's own behavior. They tested it on 11 years of minute-level Chinese A-share data. It beat the standard per-stock version across nine targets, including returns, market exposure, risk and liquidity. It also outperformed time-series foundation models and other self-supervised baselines, and did best in a historical portfolio backtest. Without labels, it ranked stocks by market sensitivity almost as well as a supervised method.
