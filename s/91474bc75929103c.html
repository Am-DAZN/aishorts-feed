AI Models Abstain More When Told To, But Ignore the Stated Threshold

A proposed idea says to tell a language model the scoring rule in the prompt. It should answer only if its chance of being right is above a set threshold, t. Researchers tested this on 12 open models of 3 to 8 billion parameters. A line allowing only an answer or "I abstain" raised abstention from 20% to 91% at t = 0.75. But moving t from 0.5 to 0.9 barely changed anything within that format. Without the line, a higher threshold even lowered abstention. Training models on threshold targets also failed to work on new thresholds. The authors say a high abstention rate does not prove a model follows the rule. Tests should also check whether decisions respond to the threshold.
