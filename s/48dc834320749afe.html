New Benchmark Tests How Hallucination Fixes in Vision-Language AI Really Work

Some AI models that read images and text make up objects that are not in the picture. This is called hallucination. One fix, called contrastive decoding, compares the model's word choices under different conditions and adjusts their scores. Current tests only check the final answer, which can hide what the fix itself is doing. Researchers built CONCOR, a controlled test that pits the correct answer against different kinds of false ones and measures the gap with a score called the Contrastive Grounding Margin. They found the methods behave differently. Some steadily favor the right answer. Others make strong corrections but also add more errors. This also explains why turning up the strength of the fix helps some methods and hurts others. The team argues that the correction step should be judged directly, not just the final output.
