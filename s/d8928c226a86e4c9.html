AI models know chemistry better than their answers suggest

Researchers looked inside five open-source language models to see what they know about molecules. They used prompts covering 92 molecules and 20 functional groups, which are the parts of a molecule that decide how it behaves. Even without chemistry training, the models sorted groups by chemical family. The base model named the right group in only 58.5% of prompts, the lowest of the five. Yet its inner representations still held chemically meaningful patterns, and most of its errors were failures to answer, not wrong answers. The study says judging chemistry models by their outputs alone can underestimate what they know. Looking inside can show whether a model lacks knowledge or just fails to express it. That helps when choosing models for chemistry agents.
