Study finds AI vision models may not need a pretrained image encoder

Many AI models that handle images and text use a separate vision encoder, a part that turns pictures into data the model can read. Some newer models train it from scratch or drop it. Researchers tested which design choices matter by training all models from scratch on the same data and compute. They found a pretrained encoder is not needed: one trained together with the language model did just as well. But having dedicated vision capacity did matter. A model with no encoder did worse at understanding images, even when made larger. That vision part also did not have to come before the main model. Adding vision-only modules inside its early layers worked about as well and could speed up the first response. The team says the best design trains vision parts jointly with the model and places them early within it.
