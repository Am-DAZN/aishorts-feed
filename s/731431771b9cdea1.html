Bad at Acting, Good at Teaching: AI Decision Models Surprise Researchers

Researchers studied "decision models," AI systems that give a typed answer, like a choice among options with a probability for each. One such model can pick an agent's actions, judge actions already taken, or score each action to train another AI. Past work mostly judged them by how well they act. This paper asks if a model that acts poorly can still teach well. On the ALFWorld test, all six models tried were pulled toward an easier subgoal instead of one that must come first. But when they judged a full run after it ended, they credited the right steps. Used as a teacher, their method, called JEV, lifted a small Qwen2.5-1.5B model's success from 72.8% to 82.9% on ALFWorld and from 56.8% to 71.4% on WebShop, compared with GRPO. The authors say judging after the fact reveals consequences that acting must guess in advance. Code is public.
