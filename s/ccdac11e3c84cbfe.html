New Score Checks If a Text's Formality Fits the Situation

Researchers have proposed a formal adequacy index, a way to measure whether the formality of a text suits the situation it is written for. Measuring how formal a text is does not show if that level is appropriate. The method scores two things separately: how formal the text actually is, based on its language, and how formal it should be, based on the context. It then compares them, allowing different margins for too little or too much formality. As an example, the authors looked at Polish emails apologizing for being late, some written by people and some by a large language model. The work is preliminary and still needs calibration and testing. Its main contribution is a general framework for studying this question.
