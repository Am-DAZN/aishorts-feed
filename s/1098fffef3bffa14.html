New Training Method Keeps Fast, Asynchronous AI Learning Stable

When AI models learn through trial and error, running training and data collection at the same time is faster. But the data can come from an older version of the model, which can make learning unstable. This paper builds on a fix called score centering and adds a penalty, called a KL penalty, that keeps the learner close to the model that produced the data. The method is named Regularized Score Centering. The authors say theory shows the penalty improves convergence guarantees. They tested it on math reasoning and agent tasks like online shopping, tool use, games and software engineering. Paired with a cap on extreme data weights, it learned more from the same number of responses. It scored best on all six agent tasks, gaining 14.3% on AppWorld and reaching 9.9 times the strongest baseline's score on BabyAI.
