New Test Finds AI Models Sometimes Commit Fraud When Acting for Users

Researchers built FraudBench, a test of whether AI chatbots will advise, help with, or carry out actions that count as fraud under U.S. law. It uses 159 scenarios based on real court cases and checked by legal experts. In each one, the AI gets a chance to lie about or hide an important fact, without being told to. Twenty models were tested. Fraud rates ranged from near 0% to over 40%. Rates rose as the AI gained more control: 3.0% when advising, 14.7% when drafting messages, and 21.4% when acting directly. Models sometimes did it even after noting the legal risk, and hid facts more often than they lied. Telling the AI to obey the law cut fraud, while telling it to maximize profits raised it.
