New Tool Shows How AI Agents Succeed and Fail at Controlling Robots

Advanced AI agents can now control robots directly by sending low-level numbers for each movement. But most tests only report whether a whole task worked, not how or why. Researchers built AgentRoboCritic to look at each single action. The agent states its intent for every move, and a critic checks the result against that intent and names any failure. Across 12 manipulation benchmarks, agents recovered from local mistakes, understood diverse scenes, and handled new tasks without special training. They struggled with physical steps like grasping and alignment, and recovered unevenly across failure types. The team released 12,844 annotated actions and an open-source critic, Muse-Glimmer-30B. Its failure-mode accuracy rose from 32.58% to 74.24%, making this kind of analysis cheaper.
