Study: Limiting When AI Reviewers Can Overrule Agents Cuts Harmful Changes

Many AI systems add a reviewer model to check another model's decision. But reviewers can fix mistakes and also overwrite answers that were already right. Researchers tested a rule that lets the reviewer replace a decision only after it shows clear proof that a binding rule was broken, plus a valid fix. They ran two open-weight models on 96 spatial-policy tasks and a 96-task portfolio benchmark. The rule sharply cut damage to correct answers while keeping most real repairs. In a later test, reviewers could keep, replace, or escalate. Qwen stopped corrupting correct answers, but Mistral still replaced all 48 correct ones. The authors say interface design changes behavior but is not a safeguard that works for every model. Restricted review is not always better.
