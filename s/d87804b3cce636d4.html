Spotting Machine Translation in Training Data Is Harder Than It Looks

AI models learn from text scraped from the web. Some of that text was translated by machines, but it may be mistaken for human work. This can lower data quality, especially for languages with little online text. Researchers tested how well tools can tell human translation from machine translation in English, Spanish, Swahili and Afrikaans. Large language models used without extra training did poorly. They spotted human text well but rarely caught fluent machine output. Specially trained classifiers did well on familiar data, but missed many machine translations from new translators or new topics, such as medical text. Feeding them longer chunks of text, up to 300 tokens, helped a lot. Poor translations were easy to catch, while high-quality ones stayed hard to tell apart from human work.
