Training Draft Models to Be Rejected Makes AI Agents More Accurate

Speculative decoding is a trick that speeds up AI text generation. A small draft model guesses words, and the big model checks them. Researchers argue it is not just a speed tool. They found small numerical errors build up during checking, and after about 30 steps they can shift what the model outputs, hurting long agent tasks. Their fix trains the draft model to disagree with the big model at key reasoning points. At those points, the big model writes the tokens itself under cleaner conditions. On hard coding tests for agents, success rates rose, with no change to the big model. The method trains only 0.06% to 0.28% of the hosted parameters.
