New Pilot Dataset Tests AI on Cameroon Pidgin and Camfranglais

Researchers built CamSpokenNLI, a small dataset for two languages spoken in Cameroon: Cameroon Pidgin English and Camfranglais. It has 228 spoken items, 114 per language, recorded by one native speaker. Each item asks if one sentence implies, contradicts, or is unrelated to another. This task is called natural language inference. Baseline models scored about 45%, where random guessing scores 33%. But they did no better than models seeing only the second sentence, so wording may explain much of the score. Leaking matching items between training and testing made transfer look better than it was. Whisper, a speech tool, also struggled. It labeled 93 of 114 Pidgin recordings as Yoruba or Swahili. The authors say speech tools must be adapted to these languages first. The author assigned the labels, so this is an early resource, not a full benchmark.
