AI Fills in Missing Sensor Readings at a Nuclear Test Facility

Sensors in very hot, radioactive places can drift, fail, or drop out. That leaves gaps in the picture a digital twin, a live virtual copy of a system, relies on. Researchers tested ways to rebuild the missing temperature readings at Oregon State University's High Temperature Test Facility. They compared six methods, including three generative AI models that learn to produce realistic data. The best was a variational autoencoder paired with a graph network that tracks sensor layout. It had an average error of 11.73°C, and cut error by more than half versus simple ridge regression. A diffusion model stayed accurate when known sensors dropped from 91 to 45. When tested on a different experiment, a diffusion model did best, with an R² of 0.91. The results suggest these models can help keep safety-critical systems informed when sensors fail.
