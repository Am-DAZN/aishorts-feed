New Method Picks Smaller, Better Training Sets for AI Models

Training large AI models on instructions takes lots of data, and that costs a lot of computing power and storage. Much of the data is repetitive, noisy or poor quality. A new paper proposes QMAC, a way to choose a smaller subset of data. It tries to make the chosen set statistically look like the full set, so it still covers the same range of meaning. At the same time, it uses quality scores to drop messy samples. The authors tested it on 4 data types, 8 models and 44 benchmarks. They report it beat other selection methods on standard image-text instruction data and did much better than random picking on several other data types. In some cases it even beat training on all the data. The code will be released if the paper is accepted.
