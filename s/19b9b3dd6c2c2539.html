Researchers test a way to explain how AI essay scorers reach their grades

AI models can score student writing about as well as human graders, but it is hard to see why they give a certain score. This study tested a way to explain a scoring model after the fact. The team first checked that the simple explanation model could copy the AI's scores. Only then did they look at which features mattered. They used about 12,700 student responses per prompt from Grades 6 and 8, and 61 readable writing features. Random Forest models, a common type of simple model, copied the AI's scores best, with R2 scores of .81 to .95. Scores for Expression and Conventions relied on many of the same features. They differed mainly in weighting: Expression leaned on word choice and ideas, while Conventions leaned on sentence structure. The authors say this shows apparent scoring priorities, not what happens inside the AI itself.
