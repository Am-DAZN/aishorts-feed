Small AI World Model Learns New Game Rules While Playing

Researchers studied how many examples an AI needs to learn the rules of a game it has never seen. They call each time a rule shows up an "exposure." They built MechEval, a test that labels 69 recurring rules across 20 games from the ARC-AGI-3 benchmark. They also built WARC, a 35-million-parameter model that is pretrained on other games and keeps updating itself after every move. On new games, WARC predicted four in five rules with at least 80% accuracy. Half of them reached that level within about 80 exposures. Without updating, only one in seven did. Paired with a simple explorer, it raised the official efficiency score from 0.15 to 0.46. On the hidden games of the Kaggle leaderboard, the entry scored 0.20.
