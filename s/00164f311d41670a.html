Teaching Robot World Models What Happens When Grasps Fail

Robot world models are AI systems that predict what will happen next in a scene. They are often used to test and plan robot actions. But most robot training data shows only successful tasks, so these models struggle when a grasp goes wrong and an object slips or gets pushed. In tests on five pretrained models, object-motion error was at most 10.4% lower than a baseline that just copies the first frame. Two models did worse than that baseline. The researchers built WorldReforge, plus a simulated dataset of 62k failed and near-failed grasps. On the Cosmos3 Nano model, it cut object trajectory error by 28.2%, and by 35.0% on unseen objects. Though trained only in simulation, it also helped on real failure videos. The code, data, and models are public.
