AdaFlowLite lets phones and cars run AI without waiting for slow sensor data

Phones, cars and smart cabins collect data from many sensors, like cameras and LiDAR (a laser-based depth sensor). This data often arrives at different times. Waiting for the slowest sensor causes delays. Going ahead without it hurts accuracy. Researchers present AdaFlowLite, which starts inference as soon as partial data arrives and fills in the missing pieces with a lightweight Swin Transformer model. It works with different sensor types and tasks without retraining. In tests, it cut delay by up to 80.4%, raised accuracy by up to 62.1%, and used nearly 50% less energy than current approaches. The authors say it can also help large language models handle data that arrives at different times.
