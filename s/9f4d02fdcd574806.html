Shrinking a translation AI model while keeping its quality intact

Researchers took Google's Gemma 3 12B model, which takes 24.4 GB of memory, and shrank it for translation in three language directions. They worked on a single H100 chip for a WMT26 compression contest. They cut parts the task did not need, like the vision system and unused vocabulary. They also stored numbers in smaller formats, such as 8-bit and 4-bit. The 8-bit versions showed no clear quality loss. The 4-bit version lost a little. They found that one quality score can miss problems, so they checked with two metrics. Fine-tuning to repair the 4-bit model overfit to its training domain. A reranker that picks the best of four translations improved both the 8-bit and 4-bit versions. On a later test, both stayed competitive with the full-size model. This matters because smaller models cost less to run.
