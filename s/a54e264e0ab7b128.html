New Test Shows AI Agents Struggle to Act on Delayed Consequences

Researchers built SMB Bench, a 90-day simulator of a wholesale business, to see if AI agents learn from the delayed results of their own choices. For example, empty shelves hide lost sales, and late shipments shrink future orders. Of 16 top models tested, only 3 beat a simple fixed buying rule. Models often wrote down the right diagnosis but did not change what they bought in time. The team then trained an open model, Qwen3.8-27B, using past records. It kept customer-protection promises more often and had fewer severe-loss quarters, but the gain in profit was unclear. The work shows that spotting a problem, acting on it, and improving profit are separate skills.
