New Feedback Method Helps Sparse Image Attacks Hit Target Pixel Density

Researchers study attacks that tweak only a small share of pixels in an image to test how robust AI models are. These attacks use a neural network that generates the changes. Today, the share of changed pixels is set by a fixed setting that is hard to predict and drifts during training, so it needs repeated re-tuning. The team proposes Density Feedback Control. After each training round, it checks how many pixels are changed and tightens or loosens that setting to match a target. At a 10% target, it kept three base attacks within one percentage point of the goal in a single run. For two of them, it also raised the success rate of attacks that transfer to other models, while changing fewer pixels. It gave no steady gain for a third attack that limits changes to important image regions.
