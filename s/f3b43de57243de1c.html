New dataset collects 77 years of speeches from Germany's Bundesrat

Researchers have built a searchable collection of speeches from the Bundesrat, one of Germany's two lawmaking chambers. Its members are sent by the governments of the 16 states, and the chamber can block certain laws. Until now, few of its debates were available in a form computers can read. The new dataset covers 1,067 sessions, from the first one on 7 September 1949 to 10 July 2026. It holds 53,362 speeches by 2,473 people, including written statements. Each entry has the full German text, the speaker's name and gender, the state they represent, their role, the session date and place, and the type of contribution. The authors say it can help researchers in political science and the study of parliamentary debate, and it can be used alongside speeches from the Bundestag.
