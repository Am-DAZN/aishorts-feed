New tool maps the structure of legislative hearings in California

Researchers have built a pipeline, a step-by-step software system, that reads transcripts of legislative committee hearings and finds their rhetorical structure. That means the parts of a discussion, such as who speaks and what role each statement plays. The team also proposed a set of rules, or grammar, for how bill discussions are organized. About 55% of California state bill discussions in their dataset fit this grammar. They tested the system on over 2,000 labeled statements from 100 hearings. A simple logistic regression classifier, with rules that block impossible labels and smoothing of tags, beat more complex models. It scored an F1 of 52.8%, a common accuracy measure. The authors say the work could help people study how power works in democratic bodies and improve accountability.
