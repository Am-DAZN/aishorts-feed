Reusing Expert Training Logs Merges AI Models Far More Cheaply

Top AI models are often built by training one expert per domain with reinforcement learning, which is learning from rewards. Then the experts are merged into one model. The usual merge method is costly because the student model must generate new answers in every environment, and an expert must score each word. Researchers propose a method called MRPD that replays the experts' old training logs instead, in the same order the experts saw them. It generates no new samples and needs no environments or reward models. Merging costs about one seventh of the compute used to train the experts. On reasoning, search and SQL tasks, it beat online RL baselines and the usual merge method. The tests also showed that the order of the logs mattered more than their amount or rewards. This suggests training logs work like a curriculum, not just a pile of data.
