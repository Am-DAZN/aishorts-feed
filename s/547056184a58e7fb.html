New Tool Spots Key Evidence Lost When AI Shortens Long Text

Language models often shrink long inputs before reading them. But a shortened text is only useful if it keeps the evidence behind the answer. Current checks look at whether the model's answer changes. They miss cases where key evidence is cut but the answer stays the same, leaving it unsupported. Researchers built SELD, a detector that needs no training and no labels. It compares how the model ranks the other answers it did not choose, before and after compression. When evidence is lost, those rankings shift. The team also made SEL-Pairs, a benchmark built from three question-answering datasets. Tested on five models, SELD found the edit that removed evidence in 83% of matched pairs with unchanged answers. The strongest baseline managed 70%. This matters because other models may reuse the shortened text.
