Reporter Tries Humanoid Robot Cleaner That Humans Secretly Steer From Afar

A MIT Technology Review writer booked a cleaning visit from Tau Robotics, a San Francisco startup. Its humanoid robots are mostly piloted remotely by people who watch through cameras in the robot's head and wrists. While they clean, the robots collect data. Tau will use it to train future robots that work on their own. Because the data is so useful, prices are low: $30 an hour at the time, now $60 a week or $100 for one visit. The robot was thorough but slow, and it stumbled up the eight front steps. It uses claw hands, which hold up better than humanlike ones. Tau's cofounder says it can already vacuum and mop without human help. Folding laundry and making beds still need a human pilot.
