Researchers Wire Exact Arithmetic Directly Into Pretrained Language Model Weights

Language models often get multi-digit math wrong unless they are retrained or allowed to call outside tools. This paper shows another way. The authors use closed-form math to write small "circuits" straight into a model's existing weights. This needs no training examples, no gradient updates and no runtime hooks. An automated compiler builds circuits for adding, subtracting and multiplying signed numbers of 4 to 8 digits. On 6-digit arithmetic, IBM Granite 4.2 3B scored 99.17% and Qwen 3.5 4B scored 100%. Both changed well under 0.06% of their weights, and they could resume normal sentences afterward. A LoRA fine-tuning baseline trained on 12.8K samples scored 42.2% and 61.7%. The work suggests exact computation can live inside a model without hurting its language skills.
