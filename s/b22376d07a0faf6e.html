New Training Method Teaches AI to Link Visual Clues Across an Image

Researchers built EvidenceConnect, a way to create training data for vision-language models, which are AI systems that read both images and text. The data comes from zoomed-in views of related parts of one image. Questions made from them require comparing, combining, or chaining visual clues. To keep quality high, an answer is kept only if several independent models agree on it. Questions are also dropped if the answer still holds after key regions are removed. The team trained models with RLVR, a method that rewards correct answers, and called the result ZwZ2. It improved fine detail and spatial perception. It also helped with embodied spatial reasoning, long multimodal inputs, agent tasks, and general visual reasoning, even though the data was not built for those tests. The authors say strengthening this shared skill of connecting evidence makes AI perception more general.
