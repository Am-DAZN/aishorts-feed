DySENSE AI Generates Satellite Images of Cities With Monthly Energy Use Maps

Cities use over two-thirds of the world's energy and produce more than 70% of carbon dioxide emissions. But detailed monthly energy data is scarce, and most AI tools only predict energy use for cities that already exist. DySENSE is a new AI framework that creates made-up satellite images of cities, along with matching maps of monthly building energy use. It keeps the city layout fixed while the energy maps change with the month and the climate. Tested on New York, Washington, Norfolk and Busan, it made realistic synthetic data using less than 25% labelled data. This improved downstream predictions by 8% mIoU, a measure of map accuracy, and cut prediction error by 9% to 18%. The authors say it could help plan energy-efficient cities. The data and code are public.
