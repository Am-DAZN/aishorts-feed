Study: Clues That Predict Training Behavior Don't Prove Two Neural Networks Are Equivalent

Researchers asked when two neural networks can be called "in the same training state." They define it by behavior: two saved checkpoints are equivalent if they react alike when training is nudged and then continues. They tested a small transformer learning modular addition. Its reaction to a short weight-decay pulse (a push that shrinks weights) was highly repeatable, so the idea can be measured. Fifteen descriptors of weights, internal representations and optimiser behavior helped predict how checkpoints would react, beyond weight size and loss. But when used to match checkpoints by future response, their apparent 16.1% edge was no better than a placebo test. The authors conclude that information a model can extract is not necessarily arranged in a way that supports judging similarity.
