Not all clinician edits help AI notes get better, study finds

AI tools now draft clinical notes, and doctors edit them before use. Those edits could be used to train the AI. But a new paper warns that many edits add details the AI never saw. In data from a real AI documentation product, over 45% of edited outputs had such added information. Training on them could teach the model to make things up. Since real edit data is not public, the researchers built a list of common editing behaviors and made semi-synthetic edit data from a public dataset called MIMIC-III. They then tested simple filters that keep only edits grounded in the input. Training on raw edits increased made-up content. Filtering toward grounded edits improved how well notes matched clinician preferences while staying faithful to the source.
