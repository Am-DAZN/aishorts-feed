Study: AI Agents Learn Their Harness, Not Just the Task

AI agents run inside a harness, meaning the prompts, tools, and control rules that present a task to the model. Researchers show that a trained agent absorbs the preferences of the harness it was trained in. They split an agent's results into two parts: the harness's ceiling, or best possible return, minus the agent's mismatch, or return lost to poor decisions. Testing an agent with a fixed model mixes these two parts together. In a web-task benchmark, just reversing the order of the observation fields cut a trained model's success from 85.0% to 19.2%, though the best choices stayed the same. Harnesses that looked worse in fixed-model tests but had a higher ceiling beat the original after more training. The authors have released their code.
