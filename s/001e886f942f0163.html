Capable AI Agents Can Teach Themselves Without a Set Learning Recipe

Researchers asked whether AI agents still need humans to spell out how they should improve themselves. They gave a frozen language model a compute budget, a general goal, and tools to play games and edit text documents. There was no fixed training loop and no real-world data. The team found that sufficiently capable models did improve on their own. Their strategies resembled how people regulate their own learning, and how machine learning uses self-made curricula, meaning a self-chosen order of practice. The notes the agents wrote also helped on outside tests, including games with different lengths and rules, and 3D navigation. The work suggests stronger models may design their own learning methods.
