Paper argues deciding which AI agents act is core to governing them

A new research paper looks at how to govern groups of autonomous AI agents. Most work focuses on making sure actions run correctly. The authors say an earlier question matters just as much: which agents get to act at all. When agents compete for limited resources, any way of sharing access forces trade-offs between fairness, efficiency, and protection from cheating. The paper proves some of these goals cannot all be met together. It then argues governance needs four separate layers: timing, state, behavior drift, and population. Its main result says that with limited visibility, none of these layers can be merged without hurting correctness or stability. Tests that removed or simplified a layer showed repeated failures. The work treats access to action as a lasting design requirement.
