Sparse autoencoders show AI experts in MoE models have fine-grained specialties

Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) models send each piece of text to a few specialist parts, called experts. It has been unclear what each expert actually does. Past studies only counted how often an expert was picked on topic-labeled data, which is coarse. This paper takes a different route. The researchers trained a sparse autoencoder, a tool that breaks a layer's output into readable features, on an MoE layer. Because the layer's output is a weighted sum of expert outputs, they could work out exactly how much each expert drives each feature. Tested on three layers of Qwen3-30B-A3B, contribution was far more concentrated than selection counts suggested. Most features were driven by just one or two experts. Labeling these features showed finer roles than broad topics, such as separate experts for Java and front-end web code, or for geometry and for arithmetic. The team has released its autoencoders and code.
