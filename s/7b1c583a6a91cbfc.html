New Clustering Method Finds the Number of Groups on Its Own

Researchers have proposed a method called Generalized Sparse Fuzzy Clustering, or GSFC. Fuzzy clustering lets a data point belong partly to several groups. But the usual version gives every point some share in every group. That makes it weak against noise and unable to work out how many groups really exist. GSFC adds a penalty that pushes tiny group memberships to exactly zero. Starting with more groups than needed, the method removes extra ones as it runs and settles on the true number. It also adds a virtual noise sink, a place to park outliers so they do not disturb other points. The idea is extended to detecting line segments too. Tests on toy data, noisy shapes, the UCI wine dataset and hyperspectral images showed it beat standard fuzzy methods. It also did better than other sparse methods at finding lines.
