Paper argues AI 'time saved' must be weighed against errors that slip through

A new paper asks what it really means for AI to save time. It rejects the simple stopwatch view, where time saved is whatever the clock shows. Instead it backs a ledger view: time saved must be read alongside the mistakes that escape checking. The author re-analyzes METR's developer study using its public data. The same 246 issues show a 19% or 24% slowdown, depending on the method, or 34% without one adjustment. GDPval's review workflow caps GPT-5's speedup at 3.6x. In a small test, AI reviewers told the drafter was ten times more accurate checked far less. They let wrong figures through in 22.5% of briefs instead of none. They were faster, but worse.
