One Training Method Could Replace Separate Stages of Building AI Models

Large AI models are usually trained in several stages, such as pretraining, fine-tuning on examples, and reinforcement learning, where the model learns from rewards. Each stage tends to use different data and different learning methods. This paper argues that the split is not necessary. The authors rewrite reinforcement learning goals as a way of fitting the model to target examples the model generates itself. That lets every stage run under one method, called unified training. Training then becomes a question of which data to use, in what mix, and when. The usual staged approach is just one special case. The team built a training system that mixes fixed data and reinforcement learning outputs in a single trainer. In controlled and real-world tests, it gave a better balance of accuracy and compute, covered more tasks, and kept broader abilities intact.
