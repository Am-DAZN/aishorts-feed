Fractal Images Teach AI to Compare Pictures Without Real Photos

Researchers have proposed FDSML, a way to pre-train vision AI using computer-made fractal images instead of real photos. Earlier methods sorted these images into fixed classes, which threw away the smooth changes in the formulas that made them. The new method uses the formula settings themselves to decide which images are alike. A new loss function, called Parameter-Aware Triplet Loss, pulls images with close settings together and pushes distant ones apart. The team also built FineFractalDB, a refined fractal dataset with more variety and steadier shapes. On ResNet-50, image retrieval Recall@1 rose from 65.6% to 74.0% on Stanford Online Products and from 56.2% to 78.4% on Stanford Cars. Classification stayed competitive, and the model held up better under several image corruptions. This suggests formula-based similarity is a stronger training signal than class labels alone.
