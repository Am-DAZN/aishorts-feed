Tree-Ising Fixes Looping AI Models by Flipping Bits, No LLM Agent Needed

Small language models sometimes get stuck repeating the same reasoning. Recent MIT work used a powerful AI agent to read a model's weights and rewrite them to fix this. It took 158 agent turns and 89.7 million tokens. The new method, Tree-Ising, treats the repair as a math puzzle about which bits in the weights to change. It solves the puzzle on a GPU with no LLM tokens and four forward-pass requests. On Qwen3.5-0.8B, it scored 54.6% on MATH-500, versus 51.7% for the agent's fix and 7.9% for the base model. It changes 13% to 17% fewer weights, and the annealing recipe cost about $0.90 in GPU time. On Qwen3.5-2B, GSM8K accuracy rose from 21.6% to 69.4%. The authors also ran a tiny eight-variable piece of the problem on an IBM quantum processor as a proof of concept.
