One Number May Predict Which Layerwise Learning-Rate Recipe Works Best

Layerwise learning-rate scaling gives different parts of a neural network different learning rates, meaning different step sizes during training. Researchers studied it in image networks trained from scratch. They found that the rate effect can be summed up as one "effective learning rate" computed from the settings, with no training needed. Ranking recipes by this number moved the chosen recipe's average rank from the 26th to the 10th percentile. Its average accuracy gap to the best recipe fell from 3.70 to 1.12 points. The benefit fades with longer training, and against well-tuned baselines the overall effect was practically equivalent in two of three architectures. A separate effect from how layers are grouped can last longer, but it varies by architecture. This could cut the cost of testing many recipes.
