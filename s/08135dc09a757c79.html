New 'check token' lets AI models catch their own reasoning errors mid-answer

Researchers propose a way for large language models to check their own work while they write. They add a special 'check token' to the model's vocabulary. When the model outputs it, the model stops to analyze its steps, find the mistake, cut the faulty part, and rewrite it. The token carries no reasoning and is discarded afterward. The idea is borrowed from ECC computer memory, which uses extra parity bits to catch errors. Today, errors in a model's reasoning chain spread, and checks only happen after the answer is done. In tests on Qwen3-32B and Qwen3-Next, the method gained 10.8 percentage points on the HMMT25 math benchmark, with about 1.13x the usual cost. The authors say it is 88 times more token-efficient than Best-of-8 sampling.
