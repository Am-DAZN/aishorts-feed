New Training Trick Sharpens Fast Image Generators Without Extra Networks

Some AI image tools can make a picture in just one or two steps. That is fast, but it leaves little room to fix mistakes, so details from the prompt can get lost. Researchers introduce Intrinsic Distribution Matching, or IDM, to help. It builds on a method called distribution matching distillation, which trains a fast model to copy a slower one. IDM adds a check that matches the average and spread of the model's internal features, using a smoothed copy of the model itself. It needs no outside feature model. Tested on Z-Image-Turbo 6B and Qwen-Image 20B, IDM scored 94.25% on Chinese and 90.20% on English LongText-Bench at two steps. These were the highest among compared methods at that budget. The team says the self-made features worked about as well as an external DINOv2 encoder. Code is public.
