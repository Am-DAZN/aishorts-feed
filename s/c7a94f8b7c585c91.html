Researchers Turn to 'Reverse Game Theory' to Keep AI Agents Honest

Some AI safety researchers think a field called mechanism design, or "reverse game theory," could help keep powerful AI in check. Instead of predicting how players act under set rules, it designs rules that make good behavior the smartest choice. Current methods, like peeking inside a model or teaching it values, have limits. Models have been shown to underperform on tests on purpose and mislead evaluators. One preprint, led by MIT economist Stephen Morris, suggests rewarding a model for how well its answers predict a second model's, so playing dumb becomes a losing bet. Princeton's Elad Hazan proposes models take turns as solvers and auditors. Cheating solvers are punished, and so are auditors who miss it. The idea is that we may never fully understand what happens inside these models.
