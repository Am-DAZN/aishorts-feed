OmniLatent builds 3D objects and their parts in one step from a single image

Researchers have introduced OmniLatent, a method for making 3D models that are already split into parts. Most current tools first build a full 3D mesh, then try to cut it into parts. That can fail when the shape has flaws or the texture is confusing, and there is little labeled part data to learn from. OmniLatent instead learns one shared internal representation (a latent space) that covers shape, deformation, appearance and part labels together. From a single image, it produces geometry, texture and part IDs in one pass. The team also added two extensions. One lets users click 3D points to segment, refine or subdivide parts. The other fills in whole-object part layouts from partial masks. The authors say it gives clean part boundaries and supports interactive editing, which could make 3D assets easier to edit.
