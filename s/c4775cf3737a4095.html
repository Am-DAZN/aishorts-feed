New method keeps AI legal-judgment skills fresh and accurate

Some tasks ask whether a person or company should be held liable, such as legal charge decisions or ride-hailing disputes. AI language models can handle these by following written "skills," which are step-by-step expert rules. But researchers say this approach has three problems. Skills go stale as real-world cases change. The AI sometimes strays from the expert logic the skill describes. And a large skill library may not fit in the model's limited reading space, so key skills get left out. The authors propose KDASO to fix this. It updates the skill library using recent cases, fine-tunes skills by studying how the AI actually reasoned, and picks which skills to load within the space limit. Tests on ride-hailing disputes, legal liability and logic judgment beat other methods. Skills also gained 8.96% when moved to similar scenarios.
