Simple Prompts Work Well for AI Language Reasoning Tests, Study Finds

Researchers tested how well large language models handle natural language inference. This task asks whether one sentence follows from another. They compared Llama, Mistral, Gemma and Phi 4 on three datasets: MNLI, QNLI and SciTail. They used plain, direct prompts with zero examples or a few examples. The models generally did very well, which suggests complex prompting tricks are not always needed. In one-shot tests, the label of the example mattered a lot, and examples showing entailment, where the second sentence follows from the first, worked best. A few well-chosen examples often matched or beat prompts with many. Results still varied by model and dataset. The simple approach also matched or beat more elaborate methods such as ICS and MCQ-based prompting.
