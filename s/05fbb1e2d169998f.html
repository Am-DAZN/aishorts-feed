New Theory Explains Why Large Training Steps Work in Classification

Neural networks are often trained with gradient descent using step sizes so large that old theory says training should fail. Yet this often speeds up learning and helps the model generalize. This paper builds a unified explanation for classification tasks, using losses like logistic and cross-entropy, where the loss never reaches a lowest point. The authors find a reference subspace that plays the role of a missing minimizer set. Sideways oscillations get damped as the model makes steady progress along directions that become flatter. They give explicit bounds on the parameters themselves, not just the loss, and show a three-phase damping pattern in the stable case. They also find that large steps can leave lasting sideways errors, which can slow how quickly the model lines up with the max-margin direction, even though it gets there in the end.
