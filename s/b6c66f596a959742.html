New AI Method Estimates Power Grid State With Reliable Uncertainty

Power lines that carry medium-voltage electricity have few live sensors. Operators must guess the state of the grid using rough estimates of how much power each substation uses. Researchers propose PoSE, a method that trains a neural network only on past data of substation power use, using a technique called flow matching. The physics of the grid, its layout and the live measurements are added later, when samples are drawn. So every result is a physically consistent grid state, and one trained model works with any sensor set, noise level or layout. On a 33-bus test grid, it cut error by 6.7-7.5% against a standard statistical baseline in all 12 settings. Its 90% uncertainty ranges were nearly right, while other AI estimators were right only 37-78% of the time. A faster version takes 0.2-0.6 seconds per snapshot.
