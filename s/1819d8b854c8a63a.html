Researchers reuse a video AI model to learn actions from unlabeled videos

Videos show how the world changes, but they rarely say what actions caused the changes. Researchers built a system called O^2LaF to recover those hidden actions from plain, unlabeled video. It starts from one pretrained video generation model and uses it twice. One copy looks at two moments and works out what action happened. The other copy predicts the next video frames from that action. Both are trained together through a small bottleneck, which forces the action to carry only the key information. In navigation and robot manipulation tests, it set a new state of the art on few-shot action probing, where a few labeled examples check what the model learned. It also transferred actions better across different scenes and robot bodies without extra training. The idea also worked with another video model, V-JEPA2. The authors say reusing pretrained video models is a promising path for learning actions.
