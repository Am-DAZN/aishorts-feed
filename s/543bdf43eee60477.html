Copying a patient's last visit beats most AI at predicting next diagnoses

Researchers studied how well AI can predict the diagnoses a patient will have at their next hospital visit, using health records. They argue that a single overall score hides important details, such as new, repeated, or rare diagnoses. With a more detailed test, they found that simply repeating the last visit's diagnoses beat most language-model methods overall, even though it can never predict a new diagnosis. They then built LONGINUS, a system where several AI agents work together. One draws on records found by search, and another uses a structured memory of the patient's history. Where they agree, the answer stays. Where they disagree, the system settles it without saying which agent said what. On two public datasets, MIMIC-III and MIMIC-IV, and four language models, it did best among language-model methods at predicting transitions in nearly all settings, and stayed competitive on overall scores.
