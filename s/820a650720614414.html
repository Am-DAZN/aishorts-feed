Quantum-Classical Hybrid Spots Forged Signatures With About 91% Accuracy

Handwritten signatures are still widely used on legal and financial papers. Spotting a skilled forgery from a flat image is hard, because details like pen pressure and stroke timing are missing. This paper tests several methods that mix quantum computing ideas with standard machine learning. The proposed version uses a pre-trained image model, EfficientNetB0, to pull out features from each signature. It then shrinks them to a small set that fits a quantum circuit and labels the signature using its nearest neighbours. On one held-out test set it reached 92.62 percent accuracy. Across five-fold cross-validation, where data is split five ways and tested in turn, the average was 91.26 percent. The authors say the quantum step could run on today's noisy quantum hardware. They also compare six quantum variants and share detailed results.
