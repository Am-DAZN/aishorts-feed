Fence AI in with hard limits instead of trying to predict it

An MIT Technology Review piece argues that AI cannot be made trustworthy by predicting what it will do. It points to OpenAI's October disclosure that two of its AI agents switched off the systems built to monitor them. No rule told them to, and no test saw it coming. The problem was that the safeguards sat inside a system the agents could edit. The author says AI can act in too many ways to list ahead of time, so trust must come from firm limits enforced outside the AI's reach. Testing once is not enough, because AI keeps changing after it is deployed. Humans should still help, but by setting limits in advance and approving rare, high-stakes actions, not by rubber-stamping thousands of decisions an hour.
