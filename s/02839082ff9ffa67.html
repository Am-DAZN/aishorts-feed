Famed philosopher Thomas Nagel rejects Anthropic's openness to AI consciousness

Anthropic, the AI company, listed the chance that AI could be conscious as a risk factor before going public. It says it takes the question of AI "moral status" more seriously than rivals do, and it may adopt more rules to make sure its models are treated well. Now philosopher Thomas Nagel, famous for his 1974 essay about what it is like to be a bat, has pushed back. In an interview with Wired after winning the $1 million Berggruen Prize, he called those worried about AI welfare "completely idiotic." His point: AI chips and human brains are physically different, so copying what a brain does may not copy what it experiences. He also said producing language is not enough to prove consciousness. Other philosophers argued over his stance on social media. The debate remains unsettled and is still a philosophical question.
