Robot Memory Often Tracks Time, Not Events, Study Finds

Some robot AI models are built to remember what has happened. But many fill their memory by sampling frames evenly over time and tagging each with a time step. A new paper shows this can backfire. On counting tasks in a test called RoboMME, researchers made the robot pause or fidget its wrist. Nothing important changed, yet the model counted the pause as work done and stopped early. Success fell from 47 to 16 out of 50 on one task, and from 44 to 10 on another. The authors say the model learned a shortcut: time passing predicts progress. Training with pauses did not fix it. Indexing memory by events instead of clock ticks recovered half or more of the loss without retraining.
