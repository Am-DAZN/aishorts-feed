Team of AI Agents Gives Small Boost to Sanskrit Translation

Classical Sanskrit is hard to translate. Words fuse together, long compound words pack in meaning, and poetry often leaves out the subject of a sentence. Researchers built MITRA-Swarm, a system where several AI agents each handle one job: splitting fused words, translating, and restoring missing subjects. On 1,000 verses, it scored 85.90 against 85.27 for a single-model baseline, a small but statistically significant gain. Fewer verses had severe errors, down from 9.0% to 7.6%. The cost is about nine times more computing per verse. The team also found that a judge model from the same family as the translators scored its own family's work 2 to 7 points higher than an independent judge, so they used two separate judges. The authors note the gains are small on a scale already near its ceiling.
