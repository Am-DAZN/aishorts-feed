DexEngine Turns Human Hand Videos Into Robot Arm-and-Hand Training Data

Researchers built DexEngine, a simulation tool that converts recordings of people handling objects into data that robots with arms and hands can learn from. It first places the motion where the robot's arms can reach. Then it finds an arm movement that follows the human hands. Finally, it fixes the motion in a physics simulator using reinforcement learning, a method that learns by trial and error. From nine human datasets, the team made about 5,200 clips for each of four robot hands, grown to 325,000 training episodes. The tool tracks objects more accurately than earlier methods that used floating hands, and runs over 10 times faster than other reinforcement-learning approaches. Robot policies trained on this data beat those trained without the physics fix. Extra pre-training on the data also helped in simulation and on a real robot.
