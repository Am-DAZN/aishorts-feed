NVIDIA Marks 20 Years of CUDA, the Tool Behind GPU Science

NVIDIA is celebrating the 20th anniversary of CUDA at the SC26 supercomputing conference. CUDA lets programmers use graphics chips, called GPUs, for heavy math work beyond graphics. NVIDIA announced it in November 2006. The company says more than 11 million developers now use it. It points to key moments: molecular biology tools from Klaus Schulten's team, work that helped resolve the HIV capsid's atomic structure, and AlexNet, an image system trained on two GPUs that won the 2012 ImageNet contest. AlphaFold 2, which predicts protein shapes and was tied to a 2024 Nobel Prize, also runs on CUDA. It matters because GPU computing became a base for modern science and AI.
