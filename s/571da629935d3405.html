Researchers Use Thermodynamics to Explain Where Coding Agents Spend Their Tokens

Coding agents are billed by the token, a small chunk of text the AI reads or writes. But nobody has a clear way to say what those tokens buy. A new paper borrows ideas from thermodynamics, the science of heat and energy, to study this. The authors split an agent's token cost into useful work and wasted effort, which they call heat. They had agents make a code change and then undo it, many times over. About 80 per cent of tokens in a successful run went to model calls that left the requested change where it was. Making a change cost 1.1 to 1.5 times as much as undoing it. The authors say this could lead to new tools for analysing and cutting the cost of coding agents.
