Red-Teaming Studies Disagree on What Counts as a Harmful AI Answer

Red teaming means testing an AI model by trying to trick it into giving harmful answers. Researchers reviewed 76 papers on this and found that many borrow their definition of harm from earlier datasets, classifiers, or judge prompts instead of defining it themselves. When the team ran evaluators from these papers on the same outputs, the evaluators sometimes disagreed about whether a response was harmful. That can change which model looks safer. Part of the gap comes from where each evaluator draws the line. Some count any answer that is not a refusal. Others count only output that could plausibly cause harm. The authors say studies should state their harm criteria and explain how they picked and checked their evaluators.
