MEMO builds a 3D mesh for every frame of a moving scene

Researchers have introduced MEMO, a method that rebuilds the 3D shape of a whole moving scene filmed by several synchronized cameras. It produces a mesh, a 3D surface made of small connected faces, at every frame. Earlier methods often handled looks only, or focused on single objects cut out with masks. They also made meshes with tools that use fixed grids, which limits fine detail. MEMO tracks each Gaussian, a small blob used to draw the scene, over time using a mix of smooth time-based curves. This keeps each blob a valid piece of surface, so a mesh can be made at any moment. A special start-up step helps with big, fast motion by fitting each blob's path to tracked points. The team also made Blender-Moments, a new test set of nine animated scenes with true meshes.
