TCP4D Learns Robot Moves From Ordinary Videos and Plans New Ones

Researchers built TCP4D, a single model that watches a regular video of a robot and works out where its tool tip, called the tool center point, moves. It also tracks whether the gripper is open or closed. The same 3D-plus-time scene view is then used to predict future tool movements from camera input and written instructions. The team also made a method that turns existing videos into training data for other robot types. The tracker had position errors of 7.60 mm on DROID and 16.07 mm on RoboLab, and it adapted to a new robot with ten demonstrations. The planner reached 69.19% average success on RoboTwin. This matters because it could let robots learn from the huge amount of video that lacks action labels.
