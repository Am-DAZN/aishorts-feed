SphereFlow Fills in Missing Medical Scans Using Geometry on a Sphere

Doctors sometimes lack one type of scan, such as one MRI image type. SphereFlow is a new method that creates the missing scan from the ones available. It maps all scan types into one shared space shaped like a sphere, so the missing scan sits close to the known one. The model then only needs to make a short move between nearby points. The authors fix two problems with a standard method called flow matching: random noise fits the sphere poorly, and the path leaves the sphere. They start from the known scan instead of noise and adjust the training. They also give a proof that this keeps results closer to the sphere. On brain MRI and CT-to-MRI tests, it reports the best image quality. Tumor segmentation using its filled-in scans closes about 80 percent of the gap to using all real scans. Code is public.
