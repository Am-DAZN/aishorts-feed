Fractal Seed Replaces Stored Weights in Tiny Neural Networks

Neural networks usually store all their weights in memory, which is a problem for tiny chips with little storage. This paper proposes Fractal Neural Synthesis. Instead of saving hidden-layer weights, it generates them on demand from a 24-byte seed. The seed points to a spot near the edge of a fractal known as the Mandelbrot set. The authors say this edge gives varied yet smooth values, unlike common alternatives such as Fourier bases or hash-based random numbers. On four small classification tests, the method beat a simple linear model by 3.6 to 10.8 percentage points. It also cut total storage 10.7 to 129 times compared with standard full-precision networks. One result is 90.30% accuracy on the Ionosphere dataset using 285 bytes. This could help run AI on very small edge devices.
