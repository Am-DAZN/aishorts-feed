DAGGER Predicts AI Workflow Quality Without Testing Every Setup

Many AI systems chain several steps together, and each step can use a different model. A mistake in one step can hurt the steps after it. Testing every combination of models is far too costly. Researchers introduced DAGGER, a method that estimates the quality of the whole workflow without running them all. It draws the workflow as a graph, where steps are points and links show how one step affects the next. It then splits quality into the accuracy of each step and the effect of each link. A math technique called matrix completion fills in the gaps from a small set of tests. On three workflows and three datasets, the median error was 6.8 percentage points. It cut testing cost by 10.2 times compared with BenchPress, a leading baseline. It also picked setups that were competitive with LLMSelector and similar tools.
