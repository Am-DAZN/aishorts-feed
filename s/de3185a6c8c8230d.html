New Benchmark and Method Improve Activity Recognition From Wrist Motion Alone

Smartwatches and fitness bands are common, and big health studies track movement at the wrist. But most tests for activity recognition mix sensors from different body parts. So they do not show how well the wrist alone works. Researchers built WristHARBench, a test set that covers nine wrist datasets and two extra motion-sensor datasets, with different people in training and testing. They also propose WISP, which combines several ways of describing wrist motion, such as shape, rhythm, order and links between axes. WISP scored 70.20% average Macro-F1, a score of accuracy across activities. The best of 18 other methods scored 59.62%. The team found that gains come mainly when a new method adds a kind of motion pattern the others miss.
