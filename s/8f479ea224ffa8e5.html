Study finds common memory shortcuts fail when AI groups make decisions

When several AI agents discuss a problem, their talk is often squeezed into short notes for a final decision maker. This paper asks what those notes should keep. Researchers built CrossProfile, a test of 480 tasks, and tried models including Qwen2.5-7B, Llama-3.1-8B and two DeepSeek models. Keeping only the rarest facts did no better than picking at random. Keeping only facts on topic let in decoys, which are misleading facts that sound relevant. Accuracy depended on the mix of evidence kept, not on the method used. The decision model also trusted facts said by many agents. Removing the agent name tags raised accuracy by 13 to 24 points in the classic setup. In natural discussions, the full transcript did worse than no notes at all.
