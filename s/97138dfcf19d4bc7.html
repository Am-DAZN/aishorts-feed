New robot system learns from failures and beats prior benchmark winner

Researchers introduced A2-MEAS, a system of cooperating AI agents that helps robots recover when a task goes wrong. It uses learned "correction codes" that mix feedback in words, spatial clues, past actions and suggested next moves. The robot's action model, A2-WLA, builds on a vision-language-action model, which links what a robot sees and reads to what it does. On 50 public BEHAVIOR 2026 tasks it scored 0.5755, versus 0.2605 for the 2025 winner, though the tests used different benchmark editions and setups. On 12 real-robot household tasks it averaged 57.92% success, against 26.67% for pi0.5. In a coffee-making task, recovery after a failed step rose from 14% to 94% at one point. The team says it will release the system, models and training code.
