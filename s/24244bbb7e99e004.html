New Safety Check Lets AI Models Learn Without Forgetting Key Behavior

When an AI model keeps learning, new updates can make it forget what it did well before. This paper proposes CABA, a check that runs after an update is made. It sets aside old examples, then compares the old and updated models. It picks the biggest update step that keeps the change in behavior within a set limit. It needs no action labels or full trajectories. In a test on human activity sensor data, forgetting dropped from 1.01 percent to 0.00 percent. But average accuracy also fell, from 93.04 percent to 88.48 percent. The authors say this shows a trade-off between keeping old skills and learning new ones. The test does not show results for large foundation models or robots, and real use would still need proof the model works well on its current task.
