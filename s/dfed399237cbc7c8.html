Small AI models get better at Swedish medical questions

Researchers built a test of medical multiple-choice questions in both English and Swedish, with each Swedish question matching an English one. Open models helped fix translation errors, and blind judges approved each change. The test showed that small models lose much more accuracy in Swedish than large ones. This matters because hospitals can run small open models on their own computers. The team then trained two small models using step-by-step reasoning written by larger models. Training with Swedish reasoning lifted the smallest model's Swedish score from 23.5% to 52.0%. That is slightly above a 20B open model in its fast mode, using a sixth of its memory. The benchmark, its edit history and the code are being released.
