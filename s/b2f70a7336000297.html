Study Tests Whether Trimming Data Features Helps AI Resist Attacks

Attackers can trick machine learning models by feeding them bad or sneaky data. Feature reduction cuts the number of data columns a model uses, which can speed up training and reduce overfitting. But researchers say its effect on attack resistance is not well understood. This study tested four such methods on five common model types, including decision trees and random forests. It used three attack types: poisoning, backdoor, and universal adversarial perturbation, on the APT dataset. Two methods, permutation importance ranking and univariate mutual information, gave the most consistent gains in robustness. The other methods worked well in some cases and poorly in others, depending on the model and the attack.
