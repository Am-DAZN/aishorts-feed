New Math Tool Measures How Much AI Responses Vary

Large language models can give different answers to the same question. How much they vary usually depends on a setting called temperature. This paper offers a more exact way to measure that variety. It uses conditional mutual information, an idea from information theory that tracks how much one piece of information tells you about another. The authors define three limiting behaviors. In the neutral case, the model is deterministic and always answers the same way. In the independent case, responses do not depend on each other once the prompt is known. In the arbiter case, mutual information is at its maximum. The paper gives examples of each. It also suggests uses for the idea in studying temperature, analyzing languages, shortening prompts, and building knowledge graphs.
