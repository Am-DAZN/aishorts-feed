New Scoring Method Picks Better AI Model Layers to Cut

Researchers have proposed a better way to shrink large language models by removing whole blocks, or layers, of their network. This is called depth pruning. The hard part is deciding which blocks are safe to remove. An older method, Block Influence, drops blocks that change the model's internal state the least. But a block that changes little locally may still matter for the final answer. The new method, J-BI, uses a tool called the Jacobian Lens to track how a small change at one layer affects the model's final output. In tests on Qwen3-8B, Llama-3.1-8B-IT and Gemma-3-12B-IT, J-BI raised accuracy after pruning by up to 19 points compared with the older method. It also did less harm to perplexity, a measure of how well a model predicts text. The authors also ran single-block tests to explain why it works.
