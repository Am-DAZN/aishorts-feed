Study: AI Forgets in Long Texts Because of Rival Facts, Not Length

Why do language models get worse on long inputs? A new paper says length itself is not the main cause, at least for retrieval, where a model must find a fact in a long text. Researchers tested five open models, from 0.5B to 3B parameters. Neutral filler text cost nothing, and accuracy stayed at 0.97 to 1.00 up to 32K tokens on one model. But filler that looked like real evidence dropped accuracy to 0.16 to 0.33. One statement giving a rival value for the same key made losses 9 to 110 times larger. The team found that the share of the model's attention left on the right evidence tracked failures best. Across 126,473 trials it explained up to 58% of the variance in loss, while length explained at most 8%. The authors call this a competition for attention. They released their probe and all trial data.
