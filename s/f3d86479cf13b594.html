Realistic Web Predictions Don't Guarantee Safe AI Agent Decisions, Study Finds

Some AI agents imagine what will happen after an action on a website before they act. A new paper asks if that is enough to make safe choices. Researchers tested 3,000 predictions from ten open models. About 86% looked realistic. Still, several models were lopsided. They rarely allowed unsafe actions, but they often blocked requests that should have prompted a question to the user. The team calls the missing piece safety sufficiency: does the prediction keep the key facts needed to choose between execute, ask or block? They built a benchmark called SafeWebWorld covering five web-safety areas. Safety-focused fine-tuning kept more key facts but made decisions worse. A recovery method, called Latent Authorisation Recovery, helped partly, but detailed tests stayed poor. Realistic predictions alone do not prove an agent will decide safely.
