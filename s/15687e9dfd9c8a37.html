New AI Sharpens Quick Brain MRI Scans, Tested on Nigerian Patients

Many hospitals, especially those with few resources, take fast brain MRI scans that look blurry in one direction. Researchers built a tool called MS-HVED that combines several of these scans, taken from different angles, into one sharper 3D image. It still works when the scans are tilted, only partly overlap, or have flaws. The team tested it on 30 routine scans from a Nigerian group, a population rarely included in medical imaging research. Seven expert brain radiologists judged 44 real cases without knowing which method made each image. In the Nigerian group, they preferred MS-HVED over a competing method in 187 of 210 comparisons. Across all groups, it scored 5.93 against 4.49. The code is publicly available.
