Fixed Steering Vectors Match Most Gains From RL Tuning of Image Models

Reinforcement learning (RL) is used to fine-tune image and video generators so they draw better pictures, render text correctly and place objects properly. Researchers asked whether these gains need complex changes that depend on each prompt. They tested a much simpler idea: add one fixed set of numbers, called steering vectors, to the frozen original model. On SD3.5-M, the vectors use 65 times fewer parameters than the RL-tuned adapter. They recovered 90.0 to 96.3% of the reward gains of six image models, and 89.8% and 111.9% for two video models. When trained directly with RL, the vectors beat weight-based RL on text rendering and human preference. The authors conclude that RL mostly nudges the model toward abilities it already has.
