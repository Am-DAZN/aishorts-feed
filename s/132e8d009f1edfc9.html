New Benchmark Tests If AI Assistants Link Visible Hazards to Personal Health Details

Some AI assistants look at a scene and also use what they remember about you. That matters when a hazard is in view. An allergy, a medical condition or a mobility problem can change what advice is right. Researchers built PReMind-Bench to test this. It checks memory access, grounding, abstaining when memory doesn't apply, and hazard response. They found models often get one part right but miss the other. They then proposed PReMind, a two-phase reinforcement learning method, which trains a model using rewards for good behavior. On a 4B model, joint success on the hardest cases rose from 12.9% to 28.6%. The gains held across several tests, including a second AI judge. The work argues assistants must be trained to connect what they see with the right person's context.
