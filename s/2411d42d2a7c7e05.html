Local Spectrum Encoding Improves TabPFN Predictions on Near-Infrared Data

Researchers tested a way to prepare spectral data, called LVSE, before feeding it to TabPFN, a pretrained model for tables. Spectra are light readings used to measure what a sample contains. On 61 numerical tasks, LVSE cut prediction error by about 10% to 15% on average compared with raw spectra, depending on the model version. On 76 classification datasets, the F1 score, a measure of accuracy, rose by 2 to 4 points. A second method, GCU, was less reliable. LVSE also helped older tools like PLS and Ridge in many cases. But in a separate test of an adaptive calibration model, gains were small. The authors say LVSE is worth testing alongside other options, not a guaranteed fix for every task.
