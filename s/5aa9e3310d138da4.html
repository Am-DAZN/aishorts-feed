Paper Argues AI Needs Better Context, Not Just Bigger Models

A new research paper says many AI failures in real use are not caused by weak reasoning. Instead, the AI often lacks the right information when it acts. This could be user details, code history, tool logs, or sensor data that is missing, distorted, or poorly chosen. The authors call the fix "context scaling." They compare it with two known approaches: training bigger models, and giving models more time to think at decision time. Context scaling instead improves how a system gathers, stores, delivers, tests, and keeps the information a model needs. The paper lists four failure types: availability, fidelity, selection, and utilization. It also proposes ways to measure whether better context is worth its cost, and calls for work on safety and governance of AI systems that remember over time.
