Researchers Show AI Decision Models Can Skip Many Layers and Still Perform

Large language models are often turned into fast decision-making systems, called System One models, by swapping their text-output part for a decision part. But the whole network still runs for every choice, which wastes effort. Researchers found that the key decision information sits in the middle layers. The last six layers could be removed with no loss in quality. Smaller models trained alone mostly copied the full model instead of learning special decision skills. So the team built a method that trains one full model together with shorter versions inside it. The shorter versions share the final layers and decision head, and learn from the full model. A small gate checks at each exit point whether more computation is needed. On seven decision tests, the method matched or beat separately trained models at every depth, with better accuracy for the compute used.
