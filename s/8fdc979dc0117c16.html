New 'Zero-Temperature' Attention Layer Generalizes to Inputs 1,000 Times Larger

Researchers built a new kind of attention layer, the part of an AI model that decides which pieces of information matter. Normal attention spreads weight softly across many options, which breaks down on large inputs. This layer instead picks the single best supporting item, a setting the authors call zero temperature, and tracks how many items tie with it. Trained only on answers, it stayed right on at least 99.95% of sets 1,000 times larger than in training. A version using soft counts fell to 42% at 4,096 items. It also solved 2-SAT and two-colouring puzzles exactly at 500 variables. Paired with a frozen 7B language model, it chained family relations exactly at 1,000 statements. On the CLUTRR family-reasoning test, it raised an open 7B model from 33% to 65% without hand-written rules.
