Brain-Inspired Spike Encoding Helps Spiking Neural Networks Get More Accurate and Sparse

Spiking neural networks work more like brain cells. They send short signals, called spikes, and could use less energy than standard AI. But to save energy on image tasks, they need to fire sparsely, and a simple method called rate coding does not do that. This paper tests four ways to turn images into spikes, plus two training upgrades. One upgrade, called Rouser, lets each neuron learn its own firing threshold. The other, K-FAC, is a more advanced way to update the network. On MNIST and Fashion-MNIST, two small image sets, brain-inspired encoders helped most in small models. Two of them produced much sparser spikes than rate coding. K-FAC gave small but steady gains over the Adam optimizer. Rouser raised Fashion-MNIST accuracy from 87.37% to 88.13%. The authors say these methods can narrow the gap with regular networks without making models bigger.
