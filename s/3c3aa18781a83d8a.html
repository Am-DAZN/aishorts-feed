Study finds interpretability tests can be fooled by 'parasite features'

Researchers looked at Causal Abstraction, a method used to check whether a neural network works like a specific algorithm. They show the test can be tricked. If a model has one direction that detects a feature and another that pushes it toward an output, the method can combine them and make it look like the model uses that feature. The authors call this a parasite feature. They tested a popular method, Distributed Alignment Search, and found it picked up parasite features in two tasks: syntactic agreement and two-digit addition. The work warns that passing this kind of test does not always prove a model truly uses a feature. It adds to the debate over best practices in mechanistic interpretability, the effort to understand how AI models work inside.
