Underwater Image Quality Scores Often Disagree With What People Prefer

Underwater photos are often enhanced to fix murky colors, but there are no clean reference images to check the results. So researchers use automatic scoring tools called no-reference quality metrics. A new study asks whether these tools match human opinion. The team tested 73 metrics against human choices from four published datasets and a new study. No metric matched people consistently. The best one, Q-Align, scored a worst-case agreement of 0.23, while two human raters agreed at 0.41. The popular UCIQE metric often favored edits that people rejected. In a test where an AI image editor repeatedly copied an image, only 22 of 73 scores dropped each time, even though noise grew 2.5 times. The authors released a benchmark called TrustUIQA, with code and data, so others can test better metrics.
