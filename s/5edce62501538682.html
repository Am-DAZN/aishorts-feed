New test finds self-improving AI agents mostly memorize fixes instead of learning hidden rules

Researchers built CLR-Bench, a test of whether AI agents that rewrite their own notes from feedback can learn a rule nobody told them. Each level adds a hidden rule tied to a named group from the Web, such as the founding members of an airline alliance. Six recent self-improvement methods barely learned with the tested model. The best gained at most 5 points over an agent that does not change. Their notes mostly listed single examples and almost never named the group. A fixed hint explaining the named-group idea raised scores by 14.5 to 16.5 points. With the stronger GPT-6-Sol model, two methods, ACE and Dynamic Cheatsheet, learned from feedback alone and reached 78% accuracy. The authors say the benchmark shows that learning ability differs across models. They released 20 test chains across 10 domains.
