Heave-Ho lets teams of up to ten humanoid robots work together

Researchers have built Heave-Ho, a system that lets several humanoid robots team up on long tasks that involve moving and handling objects. Each robot acts on its own, using only what it senses nearby. A vision-language planner, an AI that reads images and text, decides the steps. Robots coordinate by speech, with a leader and followers, so no network link is needed. The system was trained with just two identical robots. It still worked with teams of up to ten, and with mixed types of robots, without retraining. The team also built HH-Bench, a test set of 12 tasks with 2 to 10 humanoids. They report that Heave-Ho beat baseline methods and held up in real-world tests. This matters because teamwork could let humanoids do jobs that one robot cannot.
