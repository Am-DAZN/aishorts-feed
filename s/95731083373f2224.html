FOAM Trims SOAP Optimizer's Cost While Keeping Its Training Gains

Researchers have built FOAM, a lighter version of SOAP, an optimizer that helps train AI models. An optimizer is the method that adjusts a model as it learns. SOAP can learn faster per step than the popular AdamW, but it uses lots of memory and extra computing. FOAM cuts both. It drops one side of SOAP's math and uses a compact way to track statistics. The authors say both shortcuts are exact or nearly lossless under certain assumptions. In tests training language models from 124 million to 760 million parameters, FOAM matched strong SOAP and Muon baselines on validation loss. It uses half of SOAP's optimizer memory, runs about as fast as Muon, and works across a threefold range of learning rates. The authors say SOAP's gains need not come with SOAP's cost. Code is public.
