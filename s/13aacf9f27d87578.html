NVIDIA unveils Rubin HPC, a GPU built for science simulations

NVIDIA announced Rubin HPC at the SC26 conference. It is a new graphics chip (GPU) in its Vera Rubin platform, made for simulation and data work in science, engineering and analytics. The idea is that AI agents help researchers try many materials, molecules and designs, and simulations test those ideas. The chip has more than 50,000 CUDA cores, the small units that do the math. It also has a feature for sparse data that keeps those cores supplied, and a faster seventh-generation NVLink to link GPUs together. NVIDIA says this means the cores spend time computing rather than waiting. Faster simulations could help researchers pick next steps sooner. Rubin HPC is expected in 2028.
