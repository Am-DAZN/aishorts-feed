Researchers Propose One Framework to Handle Nearly All Computer Vision Tasks

A new paper proposes a Unified Law for Visual Tasks, split into Four Laws of Computer Vision. The goal is to let one method train on and perform almost any visual task. The idea starts from a hypothesis: if information about the world is captured in an image, which the authors call a World Snapshot, then processing an image is processing the world. Making an image is then generating a World Snapshot. If every visual task can be recast as generation, one approach, called World Snapshot Models, could cover most of them. The authors compare this to how large language models have largely unified language tasks. They say they tested the laws on more than 1,000 trained and evaluated visual tasks, each defined by a different input-output mapping. They also present a unified training framework for future research and applications.
