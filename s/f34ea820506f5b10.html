Study Tests When AI Language Models Should Guide Cause-and-Effect Discovery

Causal discovery tries to find what causes what from data, but often cannot tell which way an arrow points. Large language models (LLMs) can use word meaning to guess the direction, yet a wrong guess can rule out the true answer and spoil later results. This paper looks at three choices: where to ask the model, which answers to accept, and which candidate graphs to keep. On benchmark networks, the model's guesses beat coin flips. But aiming them at unstable links did not beat random picks. The tested method for accepting answers could not approve any meaningful number of them with the available calibration tasks. Learned reference graphs held the true graph in only three of 80 planned tasks. The authors say calibration size and reference quality are the main limits.
