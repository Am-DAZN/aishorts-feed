Steel strength AI looks accurate until it meets unfamiliar alloys, study finds

Researchers built SteelAgent, a tool that predicts steel properties like tensile strength. It pairs machine-learning models with a chat-style language model interface. It also reports uncertainty and can suggest new alloy designs. The team tested how the way you split data changes the results. With a random split, one model scored 0.859 on a standard accuracy measure. When whole steel families were held out, it fell to 0.113. Intervals meant to be right 90% of the time covered the true value 91.0% of the time on random splits, but only 37.6% on held-out sources. Physics-based features helped in one test and hurt in another. The authors say predictions should state which kind of data they were tested on.
