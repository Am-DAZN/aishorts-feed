Why AI Agent Safety Monitors Disagree: Flawed Testing, Study Says

AI agents that use tools can be tricked by hidden instructions planted in the content they read. This is called indirect prompt injection. Some researchers try to spot unsafe actions early by checking a model's internal signals. Their results have conflicted. This paper says much of the conflict comes from how the monitors are tested and trained. Many monitors only learn whether an agent saw an attack, not whether it obeyed. The authors split cases by exposure and by whether the agent actually complied. When trained only on exposed cases, monitors predicted an unsafe tool call up to two steps early. On Qwen, the score was 0.853, close to a simple text baseline at 0.855. Mistral-7B reached 0.896 and OLMo-2-7B reached 0.912. The authors conclude that tests should separate exposure from commitment.
