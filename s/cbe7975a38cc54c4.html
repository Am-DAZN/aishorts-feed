Spotty Participation Can Expose Federated Learning to Hidden Attacks

Federated learning trains a shared AI model across many devices without collecting their data. Some devices may be malicious, called Byzantine clients. Defenses usually assume the share of bad clients stays below a set limit. This paper shows a gap. Not every client joins each round. So a given round may have a bigger share of bad clients than the overall population does, even when the overall share is safe. The authors call this Dynamic Participation Exposure. They work out the chance of this happening in one round and how smaller groups make the bad share swing more. They also look at risk building up over many rounds, and at attackers who join only when conditions favor them. Their finding is that spotty participation does not always break the defense. But it makes robustness a matter of chance and timing.
