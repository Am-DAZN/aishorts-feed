Study Finds Neuron Groups That Change AI Answers Are Often Larger Than Needed

Researchers asked a basic question about AI interpretability: when changing a group of neurons flips a model's answer, how many of those neurons really matter? They tested every subset of 12 neurons in small models like Qwen3, Llama-3.2 and Gemma-3. They found a few neurons often suffice, and in many cases different small subsets can each flip the answer alone. The effect carried over to rephrased versions of the same question, but was much weaker on other questions. On one capital-city task, the chosen neurons barely changed across questions, so they did not seem to hold question-specific content. The work looks only at neurons, not the full wiring of circuits. It suggests that single demonstrations of a neuron group can overstate how unique or general it is.
