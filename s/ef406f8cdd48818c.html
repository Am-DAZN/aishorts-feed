Making AI Damage Predictors Reliable Inside Crash and Metal-Forming Simulations

Engineers use finite element software to simulate when metal sheets crack. A trained neural network can predict this damage, but it needs to give the same answer no matter how many small steps the solver takes. Researchers rebuilt a recurrent neural network, a model that remembers past inputs, in Fortran and ran it live inside Abaqus/Explicit. They compared a standard version with a new Consistent RNN. The standard one drifted as steps increased, so it was unfit for this use. The new one stayed stable, though it was less accurate at a fixed step size on complex paths. In a clamped steel plate test, it matched the peak force and displacement of an established fracture criterion. The authors say this is expected, since the runs are identical before the peak. They argue step-size consistency is the key trait for such models.
