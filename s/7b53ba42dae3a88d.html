Language Models Used to Track How Firms Reposition Against Rivals

This paper studies how companies change their business when new market chances appear. These choices, such as shifts in product mix, leadership or risk, show up in news articles and analyst reports. The authors use language models, the same kind of AI that powers search engines, to turn those texts into numbers. Those numbers show how different firms are from each other over time. They build a model of costly competitive adjustment, where firms shift capacity in response to changes in expected cash flow. They test its predictions with multi-agent simulation and a new statistical method. They then check the results on news and analyst reports from Nasdaq-listed companies between 2018 and 2022. The work shows how text can help measure how firms compete.
