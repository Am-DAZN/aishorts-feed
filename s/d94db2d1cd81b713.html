New Training Method Teaches AI to Read Image Details, Not Guess

Some AI agents answer questions about huge, high-resolution photos by zooming in. But they often guess from common sense instead of reading what the image shows. Standard reinforcement learning, which rewards correct final answers, cannot fix this, since a lucky guess earns the same reward as real reading. Researchers propose CrossRPO. It trains on sets of images that differ only in one small region, each with a different answer. Only reading the evidence solves the whole set, and the reward goes to solving the full set. The team built training and test sets by editing small objects in images of 21 to 120 megapixels, plus real photographed changes. Their ViRA-9B and ViRA-27B models used about 3,500 training items each. On the main test, CrossRPO scored 63.3% set accuracy versus 59.3% for GRPO, a common method. ViRA-9B is the top open model of its size.
