Graph-based AI models help match oil field data to underground rock maps

Oil engineers try to adjust computer models of underground rock until the models match real well output. This is called history matching. Many different rock models can fit the same data, so the answer is never unique. A new paper tests a fix on the Brugge benchmark field, which has several rock zones formed in different ways. Each zone gets its own graph-based generative model, a type of AI that creates realistic rock maps. These work directly on the irregular grid used in field models. An optimisation method then searches a shared 160-dimension space for the best fit, while a penalty keeps the maps geologically realistic. The matched models reproduced field oil and water output within the expected uncertainty range. Their well-level fit was comparable to earlier published Brugge studies that used other methods.
