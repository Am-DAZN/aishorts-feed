Audit finds early-stopping tricks for reasoning AI have hidden reliability gaps

Large reasoning models often write long step-by-step thoughts before answering. This can raise accuracy, but some of that extra work may be wasted. Early-stopping methods try to cut the thinking short once the model has enough to answer. This paper surveys these methods and tests them on five reasoning models. Tasks included math, science, coding, and factual questions. The authors also introduce a new score, called TITE, that measures how much computation a method saves compared with an ideal stopper. The tests show that answers can get worse at later stopping points, more than random noise would explain. Some models kept reasoning even after being forced to stop. Some correct answers also came with weaker supporting reasoning. The code is public.
