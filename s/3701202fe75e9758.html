NEO-VLA Drops the Vision Encoder to Help Robots Act Better

Many robot AI models first squeeze images through a separate visual encoder, then decide on actions. Researchers say this can wash out details that matter for handling objects, such as edges, depth and small changes in a scene. They built NEO-VLA, which skips the encoder. Simple patch layers turn images into tokens, and these grow alongside the language input through the whole model. A part that plans actions can then read visual information from many depths. In controlled tests, this design beat encoder-based versions. Fine-tuning shrank the gap but did not close it, and adding scale did not fix it either. Probing showed useful action signals often peak before the final visual layer. The team reports stronger, more reliable control across varied tasks and changing conditions.
