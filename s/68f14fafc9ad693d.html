Review Tracks How Neural Networks Learned to Model Hysteresis in Materials and Structures

Hysteresis is when a system's response depends on its past, like a spring or a building frame that does not return to its old state after being pushed. This review looks at a family of neural networks built to model that behavior. It starts with Prandtl neural networks, which put memory directly into the network's neurons and worked well for analyzing how structures move. Later versions added ways to capture damage over time, uneven loops, and speed-dependent effects. The neuro-Madelung model mixes old hysteresis rules with standard neural networks. The latest design, the extended Preisach neural network, combines these ideas to handle many types of hysteresis in one model. This matters for predicting how structures and materials behave under repeated loads.
