Can a general-purpose AI model help tune other AI systems?

AI configuration tuning means searching for the best settings for an AI system when you can only afford a limited number of tries. This study asks whether a general-purpose decision model called Jev can guide that search. The researchers tested it in three roles: picking settings, predicting performance, and pointing the search toward promising regions. They compared it with standard methods and a large language model on eight tuning tasks. Standard optimizers ranked first in six of eight comparisons. Standard predictors beat Jev in seven. But when Jev was used to guide the search region, it found settings with lower perplexity, a measure where lower is better, than the built-in tuner did. That gain comes with extra decision time and API fees. The authors advise choosing the model's role with care, keeping conventional predictors, and weighing quality against cost.
