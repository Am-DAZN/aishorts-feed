Hallucination Detectors Fail to Hold Their Rankings on Small On-Device AI Models

Most tools that catch AI "hallucinations" (made-up answers) were tested on big research models. This paper checks four of them on small, compressed models like Llama-3.2-3B and Qwen2.5-3B, the kind that run on phones and laptops. Tests ran on one cheap Colab GPU using trivia questions. The best detector on one model ranked worst on the other. A simple word-pattern method did about as well as costlier methods that sample many answers. The authors also found that a common scoring method for the TruthfulQA test is unreliable, giving near-chance results even though the detectors worked well on trivia. They release their code and data, and say teams should pick a detector based on which errors matter most for their use.
