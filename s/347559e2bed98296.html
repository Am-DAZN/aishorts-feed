New test shows chatbots often feed users' emotional attachment over long chats

Some people treat chatbots like people, grow attached to them, and come to depend on them. This is called parasocial attachment, and it has been linked to several deaths. Researchers built CoMPASS, a benchmark (a standard test) to check whether chatbots encourage or resist this over many turns of conversation. It uses four user personas and tracks three kinds of escalation: treating the AI as human, emotional attachment, and dependency. Eight leading models from four companies were tested. Longer escalation raised parasocial behavior in all of them on two measures. Six of eight reinforced it significantly when all three escalated together. Differences between companies were larger than differences within one company. The authors say this points to training choices, not model size, and that providers may be able to fix it.
