ConceptTime Uses Prediction Surprise to Explain Time Series Classifiers

Systems that classify time series, like sensor or medical signals, need to be accurate, easy to understand, and able to say "I'm not sure." Current methods struggle to do all three. A new method called ConceptTime tackles this. A frozen forecasting model guesses the next value at each step. The gap between the guess and what actually happens is called predictive surprise. That surprise marks where segments begin and end, and it describes how each segment behaved. Similar segments are grouped into a vocabulary of "concepts." A simple classifier then works from these concepts, and distance to the nearest concept signals how reliable a result is. On 17 public benchmarks, it was competitive with top black-box models and beat other interpretable ones. It kept near-98% accuracy on Epilepsy with just 1% of labels. It needed no human annotations, language models, or domain experts. Code is public.
