Dense Rewards Lose to Plain GRPO When Compute Costs Are Matched

Researchers studied how to train AI models to reason using rewards for correct answers. A common method, GRPO, gives every word in a solution the same credit. Newer "dense" rewards score each step instead, but they need extra sampled text. In this setup, that extra text made up 91% of all generated tokens. The team compared methods with the same budget of tokens and GPU hours across 1,668 runs on six models. On Qwen2.5-1.5B, the step-scoring reward led per update but ended 14.3 points lower at equal tokens and 7.8 points lower at equal training time. None of four dense variants beat GRPO. Training on prompts with about a 50% predicted success rate did help slightly, by 0.8 to 1.7 points. But GRPO with double the starting learning rate came within half a point of those gains. The authors argue that training tricks should be tested at matched compute.
