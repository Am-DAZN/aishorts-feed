ViDiHand2 Tracks 3D Hand Motion in Everyday Videos More Accurately

Robots can learn tasks by watching people's hands in first-person videos. But this needs accurate 3D hand motion at real-world scale. Earlier methods use a pretrained video AI model to fill in hidden hands, but they guess depth from appearance and fail on unfamiliar cameras. ViDiHand2 takes a different route. It uses geometry, matching 2D hand joints in the image to the known size and shape of a standard 3D hand model called MANO. It also uses information across a whole video clip. When hands are blocked or cut off by the frame edge, it blends the geometry-based result with the model's own prediction, based on how confident it is. Tested on seven public datasets against 18 other methods, it cut joint error in real-world videos by 42% and detection failures by 8.82 times versus the best baseline on each measure.
