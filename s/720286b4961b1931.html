Piccolo AI Models Help Improve Their Own Training Data and Top Decision Leaderboards

Researchers released Piccolo, a family of AI models from 0.8B to 27B in size. They make decisions by judging text and images, and they can also handle agent tools, data workflows and games. The team used AI research agents that study how the models behave, build new training data, run experiments and use the results to improve the next round. This loop is called recursive self-improvement. Piccolo-27B scored 75.59 on the Decision Index Vision Full leaderboard, and the 9B and 4B versions scored 68.35 and 67.46. All three ranked first on the Private benchmark in their size groups. The team found that older base models can beat newer ones on visual tasks with the same training recipe. They also found that well-chosen data lets 4B to 9B models rival 27B baselines on specialized tasks. Lower training loss does not always mean better decisions. The weights and tools are released.
