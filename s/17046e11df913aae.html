RefineKV Speeds Up Long-Text AI Decoding Without Losing Much Quality

Language models keep a memory of earlier text, called the KV cache. Reading all of it for every new word slows things down, especially with long inputs. Researchers behind RefineKV found that each new word only needs a small part of that memory, but the needed part changes each time. So they group the memory into clusters. Clusters that can be safely summarized get a short stand-in. The rest are opened up to their full entries. The choice rests on a math bound on error, using two factors: how important a cluster is, and how well a summary represents it. In tests on two models and three benchmarks, quality stayed close to full attention. The attention step ran up to 7.6 times faster, and decoding was 1.66 times faster.
