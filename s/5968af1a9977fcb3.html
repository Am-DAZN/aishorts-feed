New training method helps AI link words to objects, like toddlers do

Children learn which words name which things from about 100 million words. AI models trained on similar amounts of data match words to objects much less reliably. The authors say the usual captioning training never asks the model to link a word to a specific part of an image. They add that step. For each scene, the model matches words it hears to regions it sees. Words can also be matched to "nothing," since many words in parents' speech name nothing visible. No single region can take too much of the matching, which mirrors how children assume one object has one name. On infant video from the SAYCam collection, word-to-picture accuracy rose from 32.4% to 48.7%. A different model trained with a contrastive method on the same child's data still scores higher on its own test, so the authors only claim the loss improves captioning models.
