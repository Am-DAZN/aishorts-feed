AI Builds Simple Baselines That Often Rival Published Deep Learning Models

When scientists claim a new deep learning method works well, they must compare it to simple baselines, meaning basic methods used as a yardstick. Too often those baselines are missing, so progress can be an illusion. This study tested whether a large language model could build them automatically. The researchers collected 36 tasks from studies in Nature, Science and Cell-family journals. The model built a simple baseline for each one. These baselines recovered a median of 95.5% of the gap between a trivial predictor and the deep learning model's score. In 12 studies, the median simple baseline matched or beat the deep learning model. The authors say automated baselines could be a practical, scalable check as peer review struggles with the growing number of papers.
