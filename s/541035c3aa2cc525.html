StereoVLM Teaches AI to Judge Real-World Distances Using Two Camera Views

Vision-language models are good at understanding what is in a picture, but they struggle to tell how far away things really are. Researchers built StereoVLM, an AI model that uses two calibrated camera views, like human eyes, to work out real 3D space. It learns step by step, from matching points across both views to estimating depth and then answering spatial questions. The team found that training on disparity, the shift between the two views, worked better on new places than training on depth directly. They also made Stereo3D-Bench, a test with three levels. The 8B model scored 95.17% depth accuracy and 66.98 on object-level tasks. Tests suggest it truly uses the two-view geometry, not just extra image data or guesses. The training also lifted scores on five spatial benchmarks by 3.54 points on average and raised robot manipulation success in RoboCasa by 4.3 percentage points.
