Calpha teaches a chatbot-style AI model to fold single protein chains

Researchers built Calpha, an early step toward one AI model that both talks in natural language and predicts protein shapes. It uses the language model Qwen3.5-9B, which reads a protein sequence plus extra features from a protein model called ESM2. The model can answer in text or emit a special token that triggers a folding tool, SimpleFold. On three test sets, shape-match scores (TM-scores) were 0.808, 0.635 and 0.900. Compared with a baseline, results for chains of 100 to 299 residues were not clearly different. Scores were lower for chains of 300 to 600 residues. Protein training also cut accuracy on a 350-question general knowledge test from 77.4% to 70.3%. Predicting protein complexes and understanding structure are left for future work.
