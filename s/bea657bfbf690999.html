Study finds on-policy distillation can handle surprising amounts of training-inference mismatch

On-policy distillation trains a small student AI model on its own answers, with a fixed teacher model guiding it. Making those answers is costly, so systems use lower-precision math (quantization) and asynchronous generation. Both can create a mismatch between the model that writes answers and the model being trained. This paper tests how much mismatch the method can take. It finds training quality holds up under a lot of mismatch, then drops past a certain point. Some setups that break reinforcement learning stay stable here. Some model layers are more sensitive to low precision. The authors offer a light check that screens setups without a full training run. Model-specific FP8 recipes kept quality while fitting at least 1.93 times more KV-cache tokens than BF16. Pairing FP8 with asynchronous generation reached the same quality 1.62 times sooner than standard BF16 training. Code is public.
