New statistical test checks if an AI can replace human annotators

Researchers are increasingly using large language models (LLMs) to judge and label data instead of people. But when is that swap justified? An earlier method, Alt-Test, only worked when the same people labeled every item. The new AltRA-Test works even when different people labeled different items, which is common in large projects. It repeatedly resamples the data and swaps one human label for an AI label. Then it checks whether agreement among the mixed group is about as good as agreement among humans alone. The team tested 18 LLMs and 5 prompting methods on 20 datasets covering text, images, audio and video. About 100 items often suffice. In simulations, it made more correct decisions than existing baselines. On real data, LLMs passed in about half of the setups, mostly depending on the task. The authors urge researchers to test replacement for their own task.
