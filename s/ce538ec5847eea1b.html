New benchmark tests how reliably AI judges grade AI-written TV dramas

AI can now write full multi-episode dramas, and other AI models are often used to judge them. But it is hard to know if those judges can be trusted. DramaGrow is a new test for this, built for Chinese five-episode dramas. It has 800 episodes, 120 planted edits and 1,155 ratings from five human raters each. It separates clear rule violations, strong human preferences and cases where people simply disagree. Judges that gave each drama a score did poorly, with accuracy of about 54 to 56 percent and low agreement across repeated calls. A judge that compared two dramas head to head, DirectPair-GLM, reached 85.1 percent accuracy on preferences. The authors say how you run the test matters as much as the scoring rubric. They plan to release the data, scripts and code.
