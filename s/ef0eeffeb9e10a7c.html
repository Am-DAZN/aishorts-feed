New safety model flags harm in AI mental health chats faster

Researchers built a new way to check whether AI chatbots used for mental health support are handling harmful situations safely. Their system looks at three things: what the harm is, who is involved, and when it happens. Clinicians helped design it. Safety rules sit outside the model as written instructions, so experts can update and audit them. The team found public test sets rarely include harm aimed at the user, which makes up nearly half of harm in real conversations. They trained a guard model called MindGuard2. It matches the accuracy of a much larger model, GLM-5.3, with about 10 times lower median delay. It also keeps false alarms low while still catching most unsafe cases. The team is releasing the model, synthetic conversations and relabeled benchmarks.
