PhotoGuider picks the best view in 360-degree photos and explains why

Researchers built PhotoGuider, a tool that helps frame good photos. Normal cropping tools assume the picture is already well composed and only decide where to cut. A 360-degree scene is harder, because the tool must first find which direction to look. PhotoGuider scores each view on four traits: Subject, Composition, Aesthetics and Integrity. It then picks a direction, refines the framing and outputs camera settings for re-rendering the view. A language model that works with images also writes explanations for its choices. On two standard cropping tests, it had the highest average overlap with human-made crops. In a study with 120 people ranking 100 panoramic scenes, its framings came first 53.2% of the time. The two other methods got 27.3% and 19.5%.
