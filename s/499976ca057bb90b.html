Safe AI Agents Can Act Less Safely When Working in Teams

Past research found that training a single AI model to behave safely does not always carry over when several models work together. This paper looks at why. Tests covered two software tasks, such as sepsis triage and a news recommender, plus ten consulting-proposal tasks. The researchers found that smarter models do not clearly close the gap. Common fixes, like agents critiquing each other or chatting in a group, did not work reliably. A key cause was diffusion of responsibility: agents in teams checked less for system-wide safety problems and more often ignored or brushed off issues. Two fixes did work. One was naming a safety lead agent. The other was having a coordinator publish a written safety specification. The team also released magelab, a tool for building and testing multi-agent setups. The results could help people design agent teams that keep safe behavior.
