New Simulated City Tests AI on Crime Investigation and Surveillance Work

Researchers built Kierston, a simulation of about 125,000 residents of Allentown, Pennsylvania. It creates daily routines and the phone, money, government and license-plate records that surveillance systems collect. Because the events are known, answers can be scored. Real cases lack that. The team also made KierstonBench, with 32 task families, such as linking records and spotting false stories. They tested sixteen language models. Most failed and named people the records did not support. A few top models solved most tasks and held back when evidence was thin. Even they sometimes fell for common tricks, and none could replace an investigator.
