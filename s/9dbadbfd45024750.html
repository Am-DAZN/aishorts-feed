New FrontierPhysics Test Shows AI Agents Struggle With Real Physics Research

Researchers have built FrontierPhysics, a benchmark (a standard test) to see if AI agents can do physics research from start to finish. It has 56 tasks written by 42 physicists, based on their own work. The tasks span nine areas of theoretical, computational and experimental physics. Agents must read the literature, make a plan, run experiments, and write a scientific paper. Answers are checked by automatic verifiers and by expert-written scoring guides. The team tested 22 models in 26 setups. No setup passed more than 46 of its 168 attempts. Reviewing the agents' step-by-step work, the authors found the main problems were weak, not rigorous enough reasoning and a lack of expert intuition. Gaps in physics knowledge or coding mistakes were not the main cause.
