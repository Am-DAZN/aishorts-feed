New method lets image models reason in hidden math space, not words

Some AI models can both understand and create images. But they struggle to use step-by-step written reasoning to make better pictures. The authors say the cause is a mismatch. Reasoning happens in words, which are discrete pieces, while images are made in a smooth, continuous space. Their fix is called LARC. It lets the model reason using its internal hidden states instead of words. Training has two stages. First, the model slowly shifts from written reasoning to hidden reasoning. Then reinforcement learning, which rewards good results, improves that reasoning without correct answers as guidance. On text-to-image tests, LARC improved image quality, prompt following, and handling of complex scenes. It beat BAGEL by 7 percentage points on GenEval. It also got better when given more compute at test time, and did so more than word-based generation.
