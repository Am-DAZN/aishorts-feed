MMFlash Speeds Up Vision-Language Models by Up to 3.58 Times

Researchers have built MMFlash, a method to make AI models that handle images and video generate text faster. It builds on speculative decoding, where a small helper model guesses several words ahead and a larger model checks them all at once. The team found the helper guesses worse when a word depends heavily on the image. Guesses were accepted 46% of the time for the least image-dependent words, but only 22% for the most. MMFlash gives extra training weight to those hard, image-dependent words. For long videos, it also uses sparse attention, which skips unneeded computation. The speedup is lossless, meaning output quality stays the same. It reaches up to 3.58 times faster and beats earlier methods EAGLE-3 and DFlash.
