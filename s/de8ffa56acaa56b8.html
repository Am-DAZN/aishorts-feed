Teach AI what to draw first, then how to draw it

Researchers propose a two-step way to train image generators. First, a model learns what things are and mean by predicting image pieces one after another, called autoregressive pretraining. Then a diffusion model, which cleans up noise into sharp pictures, handles the fine pixel details. Tests showed the first step helps the second learn faster and produce better images. They also found that meaning matters more than how good the first-stage images look. So they built a tokenizer, a tool that turns images into pieces, trained only with text guidance. Using scaling laws to plan compute, they trained a 3-billion-parameter text-to-image model in just 6,600 H100 GPU-hours. It scored 86 on GenEval and 87 on DPG-Bench.
