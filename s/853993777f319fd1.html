New benchmark shows AI agents learn rules on computers far better than with robots

Researchers built ICLWorld, a test of how well AI agents learn new rules from examples given in the moment, called in-context learning. It covers both computer use and robot use. The test has 30 environments, 630 demonstration videos and 160 tasks with unusual rules that can only be followed by learning from the context. Rules are given as text or video. Among six top models, the best two, Claude Opus 5.5 and GPT-6 Astra, succeeded on 82% and 76% of computer runs. On robot runs, they managed only 27% and 26%. GPT-6 Astra answered 98% of robot rule questions correctly but finished just 20% of those tasks. The authors say this shows big gaps between digital and physical settings, and between knowing a rule and acting on it.
