LoCo teaches AI models to stop thinking once they have enough

Large reasoning models write long step-by-step answers, and they often keep going after they already have what they need. That wastes computing power and money. A new method called LoCo tries to fix this. It finds points in a model's own answers where it kept thinking too long. It then keeps the reasoning up to that point and generates shorter endings that are checked for correctness. The model is trained to prefer the shorter endings using a technique called DPO. This teaches it when to keep going and when to stop. Training used only math problems the model wrote itself. On math tests, answers got 41.7% to 45.4% shorter. On coding tests, they got 23.4% to 36.3% shorter. Accuracy stayed the same or improved. The authors say the code and model will be released publicly.
