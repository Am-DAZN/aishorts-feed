FedTether Defends Shared AI Training Against Poisoned Devices Across Attack Types

Federated learning lets phones and small devices train one shared model without sending their private data. The risk is that broken or hostile devices can send bad updates and spoil the model. Existing defenses each fail somewhere. Simple averaging breaks under large attacks, while other defenses lose accuracy on uneven data and miss sneaky attacks. Researchers present FedTether, which gives each device's update a weight based on how far it strays from the middle value, called the median. In tests on three datasets, three attack types and up to 40% bad devices, it was the only method that did well in every case. It matched simple averaging against sneaky attacks and beat other defenses by up to 42 accuracy points. It also held up with 50 clients, and needs no shared data.
