New Language Model Design Builds Steering Control In From the Start

Most language models learn only to predict the next word. Control and safety are added at the very end of training, or through tricks at run time. These models also use one input channel for both instructions and data, so they can mistake data for commands. This causes problems like jailbreaks, where users trick the model, and slow drift away from its intended behavior. Researchers propose a new design that keeps the two apart from the start. A separate encoder reads high-level features of the upcoming text and passes them to the model through a one-way link. The text the model reads cannot write back into this control channel. The resulting model, Mancelle-3B, steers more strongly than prompt-based and activation-based methods on similar open models, while keeping its fluency. It also resisted attacks that beat those baselines. The team repeated the result with a smaller model, Mancelle-400M.
