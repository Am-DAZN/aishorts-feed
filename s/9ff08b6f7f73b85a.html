New Reward Model Scores How AI Thinks, Not Just What It Says

Reinforcement learning is a way of training language models by giving them rewards for good behavior. Most methods only reward the final answer or the written steps. Researchers say this gives rough feedback and can miss weak reasoning. They propose the Attention Process Reward Model, or APRM. It looks inside the model, at its hidden states and attention maps, to judge the quality of its reasoning. APRM is small, at about 46 million parameters. It is trained to rank reasoning and to test what changes when parts are altered. It is also updated regularly as the model changes. When added to common training methods, it raised average pass@8 scores by 2.88 percent. Pass@8 means the model gets credit if any of eight tries is correct. The authors say this shows promise for using internal signals to train AI.
