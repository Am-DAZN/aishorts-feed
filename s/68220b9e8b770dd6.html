Study Separates Two Tricks Used in Lifelong Anomaly Detection

Some AI systems must spot unusual events, like faults or fraud, while learning new tasks one after another. A key goal is not forgetting older anomaly types. This is called backward transfer. Many methods reshape the model's internal data map around learnable center points and also score anomalies by distance to those centers. Because the two ideas come bundled, it was unclear which one helps. This paper pulls them apart in a VAE-based detector, a type of neural network that compresses data. The authors find that keeping the groups in the internal map well separated, used only during training, can improve detection and backward transfer. Whether it beats distance-based scoring depends on how the dataset is represented. Distance-based scoring is where most of the instability comes from, and the authors explain why. The results offer practical guidance on which design choice to use.
