Effective learning rate, not nominal rate, should guide training schedules

Weight-normalized training is a newer way to keep a model's weights in check, and it has shown lower training loss than the usual weight decay. This paper finds that the gains come mainly from how the effective learning rate (the real step size the model takes) changes over time. When researchers matched that trajectory, weight decay and weight normalization behaved almost the same. So the authors argue schedules should target the effective rate, not just the set one. They propose AIR, a schedule that decays the rate in a 1/t pattern with an adjustment to hit chosen start and end values. In tests on a 128 billion-token Mixture-of-Experts model, AIR beat cosine and other schedules on loss, downstream results and robustness. Terminal loss dropped by about 0.01 versus cosine.
