Reinforcement Learning Repairs AI Models Trained on Badly Corrupted Math Data

Researchers tested what happens when the first training step for a math-solving AI, called supervised fine-tuning (SFT), uses flawed examples. They corrupted the GSM8K math dataset in 361 ways, with up to 100% of examples damaged. SFT alone got much worse, with accuracy falling from 43.61% to 13.80%. But a second step, reinforcement learning with a method called GRPO, brought accuracy back to about 60%, matching models trained on clean data. Even 8 incorrect, three-line examples that only showed the right format lifted accuracy to 69.30%. Clean SFT reached 60.71%, and skipping SFT reached 35.00%. The authors say SFT mainly teaches the layout of an answer, while reinforcement learning finds the actual reasoning.
