CUA-Master Trains Computer-Using AI Agents With Self-Improving Data Loops

Computer-use agents are AI systems that operate a computer by clicking, typing and running commands. Training them is hard because good practice data is scarce, varied too little and often unreliable. Researchers introduce CUA-Master, a method with two nested feedback loops. The outer loop adds real-world material and uses the model's current weak spots to decide what data to build next. The inner loop pulls real workflows from instructional videos, then turns them into reusable building blocks to create many varied, checkable tasks. A retry feature saves a snapshot of the computer state and rolls back when an attempt fails. The 27-billion-parameter model scored 80.2 on OSWorld Verified, 36.9 on OSWorld 2.0, 43.9 on WeaveBench and 25.0 on Agents' Last Exam. The authors say results kept improving with each round.
