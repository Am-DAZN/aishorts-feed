Rewriting an AI's past thoughts in short form can cut reasoning costs

Reasoning helps AI models solve hard problems, but long chains of thought use many tokens, which makes them costly. This paper asks whether open-weight models can think less wordily without retraining. The researchers rewrote a model's earlier reasoning into short state records, then compared what it wrote next with what it wrote from the full history. Across the models tested, the accepted short histories usually led to shorter later reasoning. In a coding example, the model repeated fewer checks of the same solution. In software-agent setups, this shortening can lower total spending. But task quality and total time, including the rewrite step, depend on the model and the tool doing the compression. The authors say each model may need its own compaction policy, judged on both cost savings and how well it still performs.
