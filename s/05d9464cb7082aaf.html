Researchers Train AI Models to Argue Against Themselves and Hold Firm

Most AI tests ask a model one question and score the answer. Real use is messier. People push back, and models often drop correct answers when given bad counter-arguments. The authors call the missing skill "dialectical competence": knowing when to stand firm and when to give way to a better argument. They say it is separate from accuracy, and simply asking models to reflect or sampling more answers does not fix it. Their method, Self-Disagreement, has a model state an answer, write the strongest case against it, then rule on whether that case holds. The ruling is trained with reinforcement learning, using the same answer check that scores correctness. It needs no second model, human labels or reward model. The skill held up against challengers the model never saw in training, including stronger ones.
