ScaleVLA Uses a 552-Billion-Parameter Model to Teach Robots With Fewer Examples

Researchers built ScaleVLA, a robot control system that links a huge 552-billion-parameter vision-language model to a smaller part that produces robot actions. Most robot systems use much smaller models. The team compared model sizes from 2 billion to 552 billion under the same training setup. The largest model learned faster: with just 10 demonstrations it scored 87.8%, beating a 2B model trained on 50 demos at 85.6%. It also handled new situations better, reaching 45.1% on a hard test called LIBERO-PRO, versus at most 31.3% for smaller ones. In a real charger-insertion task, successes rose from 4 out of 10 to 9 out of 10. Despite its size, it takes a median of 216 milliseconds per action chunk. The authors say very large AI models can serve as strong foundations for robots, not just as reasoning tools.
