Small AI Models: Better to Shrink Weights or Shuffle Answer Order?

Small language models that run on your own device can save memory by storing their numbers in fewer bits. They can also be less thrown off by answer order if you ask the same question a few times with the options reshuffled and average the results. This paper tests how the two tricks interact under a fixed compute budget. Researchers tested Qwen3-0.6B and Qwen3-1.7B on four multiple-choice benchmarks. Using 6 bits lost no accuracy compared with full precision. At 4 bits, averaging four option orders won back 12.6 points for the smaller model. The authors suggest 6-bit weights, then spending the rest of the budget on up to four orders. They caution that all tests were simulated, on two small models, with the simplest compression method.
