PDRVLA Teaches Robot Models Physics Without Slow Video Generation

Vision-language-action (VLA) models turn camera images and written instructions into robot actions. But they often understand physics poorly. Video world models grasp how scenes change, yet making future videos slows a robot's control loop. PDRVLA takes a lighter route. A small Physical Dynamics Module pulls out physical tokens, which are compact summaries of how a scene may change, and uses them to guide actions. It learns by predicting future color images, depth, and motion features, with extra guidance from a frozen video world model. It needs no video generation while running. Training uses only public data, expanded with human-to-robot synthesis and extra annotations. In four simulation tests, it scored 99.3% on LIBERO, 89.9% on LIBERO-Plus, 93.6% on RoboTwin 2.0, and 81.5% on SimplerEnv.
