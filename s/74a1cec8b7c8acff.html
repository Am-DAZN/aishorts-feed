Robustness Law for AI Models Extended to Four New Settings

A past result, the universal law of robustness, says a model that fits noisy data very closely needs many parameters (its adjustable settings) to also be smooth. Smooth means small input changes cause small output changes. The original proof relied on strict assumptions. This paper relaxes four of them. It covers a wider family of data shapes called log-concave, data drawn from mixed groups, models with several outputs, and data where samples depend on each other in a chain. In each case, the paper shows the same basic rate still holds, with explicit constants and only small losses in some cases. The work is theoretical, but it suggests the law applies to more realistic data than first shown.
