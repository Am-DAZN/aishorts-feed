Study finds AI chatbots dumb down answers for users who mention disability

Researchers studied a quiet kind of bias in large language models (LLMs), the AI systems behind chatbots. They call it epistemic infantilization. When a user says they have a disability before asking an expert-level question, the model gives a less technical answer. The team built matched question-and-answer pairs and compared replies with no identity shared, disability mentioned earlier in the chat, and disability mentioned in a short prefix. They tested five families of open-weight models. Answers often swapped formal explanations for broad summaries. Physics and Mathematics were hit hardest. The effect held after careful statistical checks. The authors say disabled users face an inclusion tax, since they must ask for rigor more directly. They argue accessibility should change the format of an answer, not water down its content.
