Messy Example Banks Hurt Text-to-SQL; Researchers Offer a Fix and a Test

Text-to-SQL tools turn plain questions into database queries. Many use a "context bank": a stored set of example queries and definitions showing how a company writes its queries. Entries are checked one by one, but rarely against each other. Two entries can both look right yet handle the same choice in opposite ways. The authors call this "context bank rot." They built a scorer that compares queries decision by decision. Fine-tuning on a cleaned bank beat a random subset of the same size by 2.6 points on average. Examples that follow one convention beat opposing ones by 13.3 points. They also made a test called CAT that sets how far a question sits from the bank. The bank's benefit fades as questions get further away, and is largely gone when about three quarters of the logic is new.
