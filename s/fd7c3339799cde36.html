AI Agents and Humans Team Up to Run X-ray Experiments

Researchers used coding-based AI agents to run real experiments on a synchrotron, a machine that makes very bright X-rays. The samples were nanoparticle superlattices, which are tiny particles arranged in neat patterns. Humans set the goals, and those goals changed over time. The team saved every message between humans and agents, and between agents, so they could audit who decided what. In three experiments, 61-67% of messages involved interpretation or judgment. About half of all decisions used input from another participant, and most error fixes involved several participants. The authors say this could guide the design of future multi-agent science systems and make autonomous experiments easier for more labs to use.
