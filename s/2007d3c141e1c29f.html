New method tunes ocean turbulence model to local conditions, cutting errors

Scientists often tune a model's settings so it matches real-world data. But one fixed setting may not work well in every situation, because the model is never a perfect copy of reality. This paper proposes a fix. It builds a separate estimate of the best settings for each situation, giving more weight to data from similar conditions. It also accounts for uncertainty in the data and in the conditions themselves. A neural network called a normalizing flow learns all these estimates at once, which saves effort. The team tested it on a standard math problem and on a turbulence model in a single-column ocean model, using surface weather forcing as the context. Local tuning reduced errors in temperature and east-west water speed compared with a single global setting. The authors note both tests were built so that local adjustments could help.
