Game studio lets AI agents run development and ship code every hour

Jacob Sansbury describes how his team builds Spawn, a multiplayer game where players type prompts to an in-game AI called Savi to change the world. Simon Willison tried it and prompted a castle, then a dragon he could ride. Sansbury says a swarm of AI agents now develops Spawn on its own, spending over $3 million a month on tokens, the units AI models charge for. The agents change live code and ship to production hourly, and an hour without a ship counts as a top-level incident. Savi files bug reports herself, and fixes often reach users within an hour. Broken changes can be rolled back in 30 seconds. Sansbury says one model, Fable 5, made this level of autonomy possible, while another model fixed security flaws by switching features off. Willison doubts this works for high-stakes software, but sees games as a good testing ground.
