Merging AI Skills Through Distillation Falls Short Outside Training Data

Researchers studied a method called multi-teacher on-policy distillation. It tries to combine skills from several expert AI models into one student model. Earlier work showed near-perfect results on tasks similar to the training data. This paper finds that hides a problem. Using Qwen3 models on math, coding, agent and long-context tasks, the student matched its teachers on familiar tasks. But on new kinds of tasks, it scored 2.45-16.25% below the base model. On combined tasks, it trailed the best teachers by 18.19-24.34%. Fixing both teacher ability and data coverage removed this forgetting in the tests. The team also proposes two fixes, geometry-based patching and base-preserving replay, which improve results on new tasks while mostly keeping scores on familiar ones. The work shows these methods need better ways to keep and extend skills.
