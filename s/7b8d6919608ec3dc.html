Google's HardwareDx AI Cuts Datacenter Machine Downtime by Up to 18%

Researchers describe HardwareDx, an AI system that finds and diagnoses hardware faults in datacenter machines. It uses a graph foundation model, a single large model that can work across many types of hardware. That helps teams add new products faster. Why it matters: when a machine fails in a big AI cluster, it can stall training jobs that cost millions of dollars. The system is already running at production scale at Google. In real-world tests, it cut downtime by 18.3% on GPU machines and 16.1% on TPUs, Google's own AI chips. It also cut quality assurance time, the testing done before new hardware goes live, by 28.3% for GPUs and 25.1% for TPUs. The authors call it a field-tested way to make AI fleets more reliable and cheaper to run.
