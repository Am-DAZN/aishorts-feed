AI Voice Models Handle Language Switches Much Like Human Listeners

Speaker embeddings are compact digital fingerprints that AI systems use to tell voices apart. This study asked whether they still work when a person switches languages. Researchers used speech from people who speak both Cantonese and English. They tested 18 advanced models and compared the results with ratings from human listeners and with measured sound features. The models broadly matched human behaviour. They told speakers apart accurately and noticed when the language changed. They also relied on mostly the same sound cues that people use, though the weight of each cue shifted by language. The authors say this is evidence that machines and humans process voice similarity in a similar way across languages.
