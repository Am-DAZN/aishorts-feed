New Method Measures How Much AI Refusals Drive Users Away

When chatbots refuse a request, do people stop using them? Until now, no one had measured this on real products. Testing it directly with users is hard for ethical and practical reasons. Researchers built a method called RAW that uses a simple fact: the same model sometimes refuses a nearly identical prompt and sometimes does not. That natural variation lets them estimate the effect of a refusal. They tested 15 deployed models using two public chat logs, LMSYS-Chat-1M and WildChat. For about half the models they could measure well, a refusal cut later re-engagement by 3 to 17 percentage points. The rest showed no clear effect. The method works from logs alone and costs about $0.30 per 1,000 conversations. The team released their labels, scores and code.
