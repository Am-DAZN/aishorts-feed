Anomaly Filters Make AI Loan Advice More Realistic, But Costlier

When an AI model rejects someone, 'recourse' tells them what to change to get approved. But the search for those changes can cheat. It may find odd, unrealistic profiles that fool the model but look nothing like real people. This paper tests a fix. It adds anomaly gates, which are filters that reject suggestions that look unusual compared with real data. The team tested them on two datasets, Adult and Give Me Some Credit, using tree-based models. The strictest gate cut how often independent checkers flagged suggestions as unrealistic. The trade-off is that the advice cost more to follow, and fewer people received a valid suggestion. The authors say this shows a clear balance between cost, feasibility and realism.
