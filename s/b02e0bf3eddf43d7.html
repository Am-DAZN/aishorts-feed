New MOZAIC Test Shows AI Struggles to Spot Fake Voices and Faces

Researchers have released MOZAIC, a large set of video clips for testing deepfake detectors. Deepfakes are fake videos or audio made with AI. Each original clip appears in four versions: real or fake video, paired with real or fake audio. This lets testers see whether a detector relies on the face, the voice, or other clues. The set has 137,745 clips. The best of 12 AI chat models tested scored only 35.6% on the four-way test. They caught video-only fakes far more often than voice-only fakes, at 43.6% versus 7.5%. Detectors trained for the job reached 98.4%, but the best one dropped to 78.3% when the face-swap tool was new to it. The team says spotting new kinds of fakes and explaining the verdict are still unsolved. The dataset, code and prompts are public.
